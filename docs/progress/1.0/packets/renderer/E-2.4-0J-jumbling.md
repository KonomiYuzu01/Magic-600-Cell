## 1. Goal and acceptance
- Goal: find out whether the drawing method that passed W3 in E-2.4-01 also draws the jumbling puzzle at full detail, where pieces leave their slots, within the unchanged selection gate; and fix the renderer side of the pose data contract before the 2.5 freeze.
- Acceptance, in priority order:
  1. **Geometry.** A stdlib-only Python reference (`reference_wj.py`) composes the fixture poses with the S-B pipeline of `work/experiments/renderer-sb/SPEC.md` section 3 and writes immutable reference outputs. The GPU output matches them for three fixed cameras at these states of each W-J fixture: start, mid, end, and the swept twist at t = 0, 1/2 and 1.
  2. **Lattice agreement.** On lattice configurations (solved, and one retained-twist state built by J1 with its labelled projection), the transform path and the S-B label path give the same sticker positions and the same label at every one of the 259,800 slots.
  3. **Revision check, exact, in every timed run.** In the first frame that uses a new pose revision, the same command list copies the bound pose index, pose table and lattice flags into a readback slot after the draws. After the capture, each copy is compared with the fixture arrays of its revision by SHA-256 and element by element.
  4. **Negative tests,** each of which must end in `fail`:
     - one corrupted pose index;
     - two pieces of the same colour with swapped pose indices;
     - a stale pose-table entry with the correct pose index;
     - an adoption delayed by one frame;
     - a stale binding injected only during the capture.
     A label-only readback cannot detect a stale transform, so the label check alone never counts here.
  5. **W-J gate.** Three cold W-J runs per fixture are judged by `tools/perf/renderer_gate.py`, with the revision check passing in every run.
  6. **Re-acceptance.** If the work changes a shared path of the S-B probe (shaders, uploads, bindings, projection or shrink), three cold W3 runs of the changed build are run again and judged the same way.
  7. **Overlays.** Admissible and blocked grips, the straddling pieces with their two certificate points, and the twist angle during preview are drawn from the fixture data. Each overlay's frame-time cost is measured alone in W-J, as for the H-06 table. An overlay not measured by the time box is listed as unmeasured, never estimated.
- Non-goals: UI, input beyond camera rotation, Look Lab presets, the final look of the overlays (design track, G1–G6 stay with the owner), deciding legality, NVIDIA features, shipping code quality.

## 2. Actual problem and reproduction
- The selection gate was measured on W3, a retained turn. Every retained configuration keeps each piece in a slot, so the S-B method uploads 259,800 labels and draws fixed slot geometry.
- The jumbling puzzle is a separate puzzle with its own model identity (owner decision, 9 October 2026), entered as an optional state of a session (Jumble on or off, owner decision 7): the same scene switches between the label path and the pose path at a lattice state, which acceptance item 2 makes invisible. Its twists are rigid rotations of a cap that are not in the retained group. After them:
  - pieces leave their slots, so the label-buffer method alone cannot draw the state;
  - the renderer needs a rigid four-dimensional transform per piece, for up to 177,120 pieces;
  - the outer shape is no longer the polytope;
  - retained turns also pass through off-slot poses while they animate.
- Observed scale in the three scripted scrambles (J1 replays, `research/jumbling/fixtures/`), at the end of each script:

  | Fixture (menu) | Twists | Pieces off the lattice | Pose-table entries (in K⁺) | Blocked grips | Swept twist: grip, angle, moving pieces |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
  | `wj-S4` (S4₀) | 640 | 117,498 of 177,120 | 1,591 (386) | 578 of 600 | 209, 90°, 2,197 |
  | `wj-I_a` (I_a) | 841 | 121,184 | 1,333 (268) | 573 | 564, 72°, 2,926 |
  | `wj-I_b` (I_b) | 885 | 128,013 | 1,248 (242) | 563 | 249, 144°, 2,882 |

  These are observed workloads, not proved worst cases. Whether the number of poses is bounded for any of these menus is open (J3 item 3).
- Reproduce the fixture state: `python research/jumbling/fixtures/wj.py export S4 end <dir>` writes the arrays of section 4 with a header that names their SHA-256 digests; `check` replays a whole fixture with J1 and compares every reference.

## 4. Necessary source and evidence
- **Geometry** is that of 600-cell-Full, unchanged: the five workload assets of E-2.4-01 (`assets/mesh_vertices.f32`, `mesh_sticker.u32`, `mesh.json`, `mesh_centers.f32`, `cell_frames.f32`) and `assets/slot_piece.u32` (slot to piece). A sticker slot `s` belongs to piece `slot_piece[s]`, and all stickers of a piece move together.
- **Fixtures** (`research/jumbling/fixtures/wj-<menu>.json`, menus S4, I_a and I_b): the exact menu record; the J1 journal with model identity, menu identity and contract revision; references at the stages start, mid and end; the swept twist; the end survey. Each stage records the exact J1 digest, counts, the SHA-256 of the three arrays below and 48 sampled pieces with their posed centroid in the asset frame (float64). The swept twist records its grip, angle, moving set (count and SHA-256), the rotation plane (`plane_u`, `plane_v`, unit four-vectors) and 16 sampled moving pieces at t = 0, 1/2 and 1. The end survey gives the status of all 600 grips, and for each blocked grip the straddling piece and its two exact certificate points with float copies.
- **Render data contract:** `research/jumbling/render-contract.md` (`magic600-jumbling-render/1`). It is the only interface between the engine side and any drawing, and it does not fix the dimension: every header names d, a pose is d × d and the projection is a chain of stages. 1.0 implements and accepts d = 4 only (owner decision 6, 9 October 2026); this packet carries the rendering acceptance. The full-state research page (`research/jumbling/viewer/full.html`) reads the same contract and the same assets, so it can serve as a reference picture.
- **Arrays** written by `wj.py export <menu> <stage> <dir>` (stages `start`, `mid`, `end`, `sweep-before`, `sweep-after`), little-endian:
  - `pose_index.i32`: 177,120 pose ids, one per piece. Pose 0 is the identity.
  - `poses.f32`: one 4 × 4 float32 matrix per pose, row-major, acting on column vectors (x′ = M x) in the asset frame.
  - `pose_lattice.i32`: the K⁺ index of each pose, or −1. The lattice flag of a piece is `pose_lattice[pose_index[piece]] >= 0`.
  - `header.json`: counts, the J1 digest and the SHA-256 of each file.
- **Pipeline change** against SPEC.md section 3: the sticker and cell shrink stay anchored in the home frame (steps 1–3 unchanged). Then `world = M[pose_index[slot_piece[slot]]] · world`. M is orthogonal, so the 1/R scaling of step 2 commutes with it. A moving piece in a swept twist then gets the turn of step 4 with the fixture's `plane_u`, `plane_v` and angle. Steps 5–7 are unchanged. The fixture centroids are in the asset frame; divide by R = `normal_length` to compare with world coordinates.
- **Frame check.** The poses act in the frame of the meshes only if `F[c] · (N0 / R)` equals the unit pole of cell c from `assets/model.npz` (`normals`) for all 600 cells, and R equals the pole length. In this session that holds to within 1e-8 (R = 4.5765); `check_wj.py` repeats the check.
- **W-J trace** (per fixture): full detail at the fixture's `sweep-before` state; the swept twist and its exact inverse alternate back to back for the whole capture with the W3 timing of SPEC.md (190 ms smoothstep turns), and the W2 camera rotation runs every frame. The inverse is admissible from the state after the twist with the same moving set (theory draft, Proposition 1.2), so the trace is legal at every step. Pose revision t is the number of completed turns: even revisions are the `sweep-before` arrays, odd ones the `sweep-after` arrays. At each adoption the CPU writes the whole pose index and pose table; a probe may upload less only if the revision check still covers the whole bound data.
- **Overlays:** the end survey and its certificate points are the only source of blocked grips and straddle points. Certificate points can lie on no sticker, so they never come from the meshes.
- **Correctness boundary:** rendering never decides legality, never changes mechanical state and never relabels pieces. The engine side (J1 now, the product engine later; ENG-0J) is the source of truth for poses, revisions, lattice flags, grip status and certificates.
- **Gate** and judge: the README of this folder ("Fixed inputs every packet repeats"), unchanged, with `tools/perf/renderer_gate.py`. W-J is a new scene; the judge's run format may need a scene value `wj` and a `pose_check` record beside `label_check`. Any change to the judge goes through its own review and keeps its tests; it never relaxes a threshold.

## 6. Constraints and owned files
- Never change `assets/`, model identity, labels or mechanical state, the S-B probe source or `research/jumbling/`. Read assets through their manifest digests and fixtures through their SHA-256 digests.
- No personal session; synthetic states only (the fixtures).
- Owned files: `work/experiments/renderer-wj/**` only. Start from a copy of the S-B probe. No Managed DirectX DLLs; nothing under `native/` changes.
- NVIDIA-specific features stay off and out of the build.
- A geometry change (cut depth, piece or sticker meshes) is a new model identity for both puzzles and re-runs the whole renderer acceptance, as the owner said. A menu change replaces the fixtures and re-runs W-J.
- Time box: 1.5 days. Early pass: when one transform method passes W-J, stop trying others, then complete items 1–4, 6 and as much of 7 as the time box allows. Early failure: when two transform methods both stay below 50 % of the gate on W-J, stop optimising, record the figures and escalate the drawing method.
- `work/experiments/renderer-wj/check_wj.py` (no GPU, no Windows) first runs `tools/perf/check_renderer_assets.py`, then the frame check of section 4, then `wj.py export` for the stages it needs of every fixture (which replays the J1 journal and checks the array digests), then compares the outputs of `reference_wj.py` with the committed ones byte for byte.
- `check_wj.py` writes every export and every recomputed reference into a fresh temporary directory outside the repository and deletes it, on failure too. The wrapper refuses a run when the acceptance check leaves or changes any file in the worktree, ignored files included, and Codex keeps the worktree free of generated output during its work as well (the first full-state viewer call, `20261009T221226Z-dc63d4b2`, was refused for exactly this).
