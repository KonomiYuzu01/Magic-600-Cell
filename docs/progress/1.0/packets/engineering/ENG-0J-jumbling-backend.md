Jumbling is an optional state of a puzzle session (owner decision 7, 9 October 2026): Jumble is off by default, and while it is off every layer behaves and stores exactly as for 600-cell-Full alone. 600-cell-Full and its contract are unchanged. Revision 2 adds the observation data, checks, lists, stored words and crossings that the owner decisions of 13 to 22 October 2026 allow, and nothing that the DT-J1 filter put out.

## 2. Actual problem and reproduction
- 1.0 has two puzzles (owner decision, 9 October 2026): 600-cell-Full, whose state is a labelled slot permutation of 259,800 stickers, and the jumbling puzzle on the same geometry, whose state is an exact pose per piece. The six-layer architecture of charter section 4 was drawn for the first only:
  - the engine is "proven equal to 0.4 by the differential oracle", but 0.4 has no jumbling state;
  - the session store keeps a journal of retained moves and label checkpoints;
  - the command layer knows retained operations and macros;
  - the renderer reads labels only.
- What the jumbling puzzle needs instead (contract sections 1–5 and amendments A1–A4, with A5 proposed here):
  - **State.** 177,120 pieces, each with an exact 4 × 4 rotation over Q(√5). Poses are interned in a pose table; a pose in K⁺ (7,200 elements) is stored by its index, any other by its exact matrix. Equality of states is exact pose equality, with a canonical digest. Entry heights are unbounded under I_a (X1).
  - **Twists.** A grip (one of 600 poles) and an exact rotation fixing it. The exact domain and the bounded input map of A1, and the signed menu I_a of A4. A rotation without an exact representation is rejected, never approximated.
  - **Admissibility.** Exact inside/outside/straddle classification against the cut hyperplane, with certificates: a straddling piece is shown by two exact points on opposite sides. Uncertain means rejected. A commit is all-or-nothing. A blocked cap is blocked by its whole straddler set, which can be large (44 straddlers at cap 150 on one recorded state; DT-J1 M2), not by one piece.
  - **Checkpoints.** Handoff to the retained solving workflow from a witnessed retained-state checkpoint (contract section 5) or, under A5, from a label checkpoint. Reaching the lattice is not enough by itself: a lattice state reached through jumble twists can have ν ≠ 0, and then no retained word reaches its labels (card 2 answered by a counterexample; stage 04: the values of ν form C2^34 × C5^2). At ν = 0 the labels are a retained state (G = 𝒦, stage 03), but a witness word that reaches the exact poses is in practice never at hand before the pose-solved end, because its inverse would be a solving word (DT-J1 M17). This is the gap EH-18 names.
- **Jumbling as an optional state** (owner decision 7, 9 October 2026). A puzzle session is a 600-cell-Full session that may enter and leave jumbling, not a different kind of session. The two crossings, as revision 2 proposes them:
  - **Jumble on.** The labelled state alone does not give the poses: the projection π of amendment A3 is not injective, so several pose states share one labelling. Entering therefore replays, through the jumbling engine, either the retained word from solved that the session's journal holds for the current state, or, after an earlier crossing, the retained moves made since then from the stored exit poses (A5). Labels alone never suffice: any lift of the labels behaves the same at every later twist (A5's lemma), but its pose digest is not determined.
  - **Jumble off.** At a label checkpoint (A5): N = 0 and ν = 0. The labels go back to 600-cell-Full, which continues from that label checkpoint, and the exit poses are stored with the episode. A witnessed retained-state checkpoint is the special case with a word.
  - **While on.** Every twist, retained ones included, goes through the jumbling engine and its admissibility check. The retained workflow's tools (macros, block building, retained certificates, setup search) need Jumble off (owner, O8 (1): they run after the crossing). Menu words, the solver's own and the 36 corrections, run only while Jumble is on. The menu, and with it the jumbling model identity, stays fixed for one episode; I_a is the only signed menu, so a menu change is a refused case, not a workflow.
- **What the research of 10 to 20 October adds that the layers must respect** (handoff observation log):
  - one stored word can have hundreds of thousands of twists (672,417 on record), so the store, the replay and the preview take no length limit from the theory (EO-01, EO-06, EO-16);
  - N changes only along the cut of the twisted cap, and N = 0 exactly on the lattice, so the rim summary of 600 rows carries the cap map and every N check (EO-02);
  - steps computed at one state need not compose, and stored certificates, seeds and hole counts do not carry over between states, so a stored word is re-checked whole at the current revision before it commits (EO-07, EO-17, EH-24);
  - the inverse of an admissible twist is admissible with the same inside set, so a committed word can be undone twist by twist (EO-13);
  - nothing may rest on a bound for the off-lattice phase or on a catalogue having a step; the program runs no step search, and a job that finds nothing reports what it searched (EO-14, EO-19);
  - a composed check is not a replay, and a fast path for long words needs an agreement test against per-twist replay before it is trusted (EO-21, EO-22);
  - research tools already read an unfinished or mismatched result as "stuck" once (EO-23, EO-24).
- Observed scale (J1 replays, `research/jumbling/fixtures/`), at the end of each scripted scramble:

  | Fixture (menu) | Twists | Pieces off the lattice | Pose-table entries (in K⁺) | Largest entry height | Blocked grips | Replay with references | End survey |

  | `wj-S4` (S4₀) | 640 | 117,498 of 177,120 | 1,591 (386) | 32 | 578 of 600 | 380 s | 61 s |

  Heights are the largest |numerator| or denominator of an entry of a non-K⁺ pose, in the form (a + b√5)/d. Times are cloud timings of the single-threaded Python reference while five other jobs shared a four-core container; they include building the references and are not performance evidence. In the random walks that produced these scripts, 93–96% of attempted twists were blocked once the state was scrambled, so a product scramble runs as a job (EO-12). Heights do not stay bounded under I_a: R(I_a) is infinite (X1, proved 17 October 2026; EH-04), and one letter changes a piece's left height by at most 2 and never its right height (EH-29).
- Reproduce: `python research/jumbling/fixtures/wj.py check` replays every fixture with J1 and compares every reference; `python research/jumbling/sim/accept.py` reruns the J1 acceptance. The order digest of section 4 is recomputed from the fixture's `menu` record with the standard library alone.

## 8. Proposed amendment A5: label checkpoints and the end of a solve

Proposed by the integrator on 22 October 2026 for the owner's answer O8 (1). After the Astra plan check of this revision it enters section 7 of the state contract as written here, with the plan check's fixes; where it differs from section 5, it holds.

### A5 Label checkpoints
- **ν.** On a lattice configuration X, ν(X) is the invariant of the labelled state π(X) of A3: 35 sign bits b0 to b34, 8 frame bits f0, f3, f6, f8, f11, f22, f25 and f32, and two values modulo 5, C5_17 and C5_28 (`research/jumbling/manual/04-lattice.md` section 4.3; reference `research/jumbling/restoration/03-retained-group-and-lattice-restorer/scripts/nucoords.py`). ν is defined on lattice configurations only.
  - *C5 convention.* Each C5 value is read in the generator r0 that `nucoords.py` picks: the lexicographically smallest order-5 permutation built from `assets/execution_trees.json.gz` and `assets/seed_atlas.json`, both under the model identity. With another generator every C5 value is multiplied by a unit modulo 5 (DT-J1 M13), so the convention is part of the definition. The sign and frame bits need none (Lemma 8 of the retained theory).
  - *Fixed coordinates.* b0, b1, b2, b3, b5, b6, f0, f3 and f6 are 0 on every reachable lattice configuration (stage 04). A nonzero value there is a consistency error of the engine, never a state.
- **Label checkpoint.** A reachable lattice configuration X is a label checkpoint when ν(X) = 0. Its certificate records the exact lattice test (every pose in K⁺, every centre in its own chamber), the frame agreement of π(X) (A3), ν(X) = 0 under the C5 convention, and the theorem it rests on: the retained group G equals the kernel 𝒦 of ν (card 1, an accepted theorem, with the 46 certificates of stage 03, which an independent checker passes; certificate program `research/jumbling/restoration/03-retained-group-and-lattice-restorer/certificates/certificates.json.gz`). So π(X) is a labelled state that some legal word in the 1,200 generators reaches from solved (stage 03 report, section 2.4). No such word is produced, stored or searched for.
  - *Reachability.* The certificate uses that X is reachable, as Theorem R of stage 03 does: a lattice configuration that is not reachable can have ν = 0 and displaced centres (stage 03 report, section 3.2, scope). The engine reaches X only through admissible twists from solved, or from a stored exit state that was reached so, and the store keeps the journal that shows it. A pose state read without that journal is not a label checkpoint.
  - A retained-state checkpoint of section 5 is a label checkpoint: a retained word keeps ν = 0 (card 1, F1).
- **What a label checkpoint certifies.** It certifies the labels: every tool and certificate that section 5 lists and that reads labelled states and legal words (orbit-first block building; R, F, I, C and E; parity and orientation sums; controller and setup tables) applies to π(X). It does not certify the poses: X can differ from every pose state that a retained word reaches, by rotations in K⁺ that fix a piece's home chamber and each of its sticker regions (A3: π is not injective). No later twist can see such rotations:
  - *Lemma (label lift).* Let X and Y be lattice configurations with π(X) = π(Y). For every piece A, s_A = Y[A]⁻¹ X[A] lies in K⁺, maps Ā₀ onto itself and maps each sticker region of A onto itself. Then every word admissible from Y is admissible from X with the same inside sets at every prefix, and after it X′[A] = Y′[A] s_A for every piece A. So posed regions, admissibility, inside sets, N and, on the lattice, labels agree at every later revision.
  - *Proof.* Equal labels put each sticker of A in the same slot under X[A] and Y[A], so both poses map A's chamber to one chamber and each sticker region of A to one sticker region; hence s_A, an element of K⁺, maps Ā₀ and each sticker region of A onto itself. If X[A] = Y[A] s_A, the posed regions agree: X[A](Ā₀) = Y[A](s_A(Ā₀)) = Y[A](Ā₀). Admissibility and inside sets depend only on posed regions (section 3), and an admissible twist multiplies the poses of its inside pieces on the left, which keeps the relation. Sticker regions agree for the same reason. Induction on the prefixes gives the claim (DT-J1 M10, M17).
  - The stored exit poses therefore serve the digest, the journal and replay, never a visible fact.
- **Jumble off.** Jumble off is allowed exactly at label checkpoints, every retained-state checkpoint among them. It hands π(X) to 600-cell-Full as a label checkpoint of its journal, keeps the certificate with the episode (the pose digest of X, a digest of π(X), ν = 0, the C5 convention, the SHA-256 of the certificate program and the contract revision), and stores the exit pose state X exactly (pose table and pose ids).
- **The end of a solve.** A jumbled solve ends on the sticker test: N = 0 and every one of the 259,800 slots carries its home label, that is, π(X) is the identity (owner, 22 October 2026, O8 (1)). Such an X is a label checkpoint whatever its poses. Pose equality with solved is not an end test and is not shown as a separate fact. Contract equality stays exact pose equality (section 2, A3): the engine keeps pose digests for replay, undo and checkpoints.
- **Jumble on after a crossing.** Jumble on replays, through the jumbling engine, the retained moves that 600-cell-Full's journal holds since the last crossing, starting from the stored exit poses. Each of them is admissible, because every reachable lattice configuration has the solved shape and admissibility depends only on the shape (stage 03 report, section 3.2, from card 2, Lemma 2). The labels of the entered state equal 600-cell-Full's, because retained twists act on π as the 1,200 generators act on labels (J1's acceptance against `rotperms`, `move_src` and `move_dst`), and the entered state is a label checkpoint again. When 600-cell-Full's labels since the crossing were set by anything other than its journal moves (a reset, an import, a restore of an earlier checkpoint), the exit record no longer applies, and Jumble on needs a retained word from solved, as before. Labels alone are refused.
- **Refusals.** Jumble off is refused with a typed error and no state change off the lattice (N > 0) and at ν ≠ 0. At ν ≠ 0 no retained word reaches π(X), because retained twists keep ν = 0. The message names the fact (pieces off the lattice; ν is not 0) and the kind of action (twists that bring pieces back; corrections), never which correction or twist (DT-J1 A5).
- **Section 5, as amended.** Its first bullet applies to the labels from a label checkpoint. "Returning to the lattice is not enough" now reads: returning to the lattice is enough for the labels exactly when ν = 0. The audit bullet is answered: jumbling reaches lattice states outside G, and ν detects exactly those (card 2 answered by a counterexample; stage 04). The human-solve boundary is unchanged: no witness word and no solving word is searched for, and protection stays pose-based inside Jumble (A3) and is 600-cell-Full's after the crossing.
