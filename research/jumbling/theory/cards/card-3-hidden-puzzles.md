# Card 3: five ordinary puzzles inside the icosahedral jumbling puzzle

Open problem R1 of the jumbling theory (restoring deep configurations), in its pure-mathematics part. Prove Theorems A to D below. Each has a short proof from the definitions; Theorem D is conditional on finite certificates, which you should state exactly. Question E at the end is optional. Label every statement proved, conditional (name the condition) or conjectured, and name every machine check as a certificate with its exact statement; do not claim that a certificate holds.

## Model

**Quaternions.** Identify $\mathbb R^4$ with the quaternions $\mathbb H$ (basis $1,i,j,k$). $2I\subset\mathbb H$ is the binary icosahedral group: the 120 unit quaternions $\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k$, $\tfrac12(\pm1\pm i\pm j\pm k)$ and the even coordinate permutations of $\tfrac12(\pm\varphi,\pm1,\pm\varphi^{-1},0)$, $\varphi=\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}2$. Write $[a,b]$ for the rotation $x\mapsto a\,x\,b^{-1}$ ($a,b$ unit quaternions); $[a,b]=[-a,-b]$. $K^+=\{[u,v]:u,v\in2I\}$ is the rotation group of the 600-cell with vertex set $2I$ (order 7200).

**Grips.** The 600 unit poles $\hat n_c$ are the unit vectors towards the facet centres of the 600-cell $2I$; they are the vertices of a regular 120-cell, and $K^+$ permutes them transitively. The polytope $\widetilde P$ is the 600-cell with facets $\{x:n_c\cdot x=\|n_c\|^2\}$, $n_c=\rho\,\hat n_c$ for a fixed $\rho>0$. Grip $c$ has the cut hyperplane $H_c=\{x:n_c\cdot x=\alpha\|n_c\|^2\}$ with $\alpha=121/125$ and the cap $H_c^+=\{x:n_c\cdot x\ge\alpha\|n_c\|^2\}$.

**Chambers and pieces.** The 600 cuts divide $\operatorname{int}\widetilde P$ into chambers. The core (below every cut) never moves; each of the other $177{,}120$ chambers is the home region $\bar A_0$ of one piece $A$.

**Configurations and twists.** A configuration $X$ gives every piece a pose $X[A]\in SO(4)$ and the posed region $X[A](\bar A_0)$. $X$ is valid when the interiors of its posed regions and of the core are pairwise disjoint. A twist $(c,g)$ with $g\in\Lambda_c$ (the menu, below) is admissible from $X$ when no posed region straddles $H_c$ (has interior points strictly on both sides); it then left-multiplies by $g$ the pose of every piece whose posed region lies in $H_c^+$ and leaves the others. Reachable configurations are those produced by admissible twists from the solved configuration (every pose the identity); they are valid. A piece is on the lattice when its pose lies in $K^+$; then its region is a chamber. Pieces whose poses lie in one left coset $\gamma K^+$ form a domain.

**The menu $I_a$.** $\Lambda_c=\{[\hat n_c u\hat n_c^{-1},\,u]:u\in2I\}$, an icosahedral group of order 60 fixing $\hat n_c$; its intersection with $K^+$ is the tetrahedral group $A4_c$ (the retained turns of grip $c$).

## Computed facts (exact computations in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$; use as facts)

- **F1.** The 600 poles split into five classes $\mathcal P_0,\dots,\mathcal P_4$ of 120: $\mathcal P(c)=\hat n_c\cdot2I$, the class of $c$. Each class is the vertex set of a regular 600-cell. $K^+$ maps classes onto classes and acts on the five classes as $A_5$ (60 even permutations); the stabiliser of a class in $K^+$ has order 1440.
- **F2.** For every grip $c$, $\mathcal P(c)$ is exactly the set of poles $d$ such that $g\hat n_d$ is a pole for every $g\in\Lambda_c$: for each of the 600 grips and each pole $d$ outside $\mathcal P(c)$ some $g\in\Lambda_c$ maps $\hat n_d$ to a non-pole, and $\Lambda_c$ maps $\mathcal P(c)$ onto itself (immediate: $\hat n_cu\hat n_c^{-1}\cdot\hat n_cv\cdot u^{-1}=\hat n_c\,uvu^{-1}$). The menu used in the computations equals $\Lambda_c$ as defined above at all 600 grips.
- **F3.** For each class, the cuts of that class divide $\operatorname{int}\widetilde P$ into 1,441 cells (pieces grouped by which caps of the class contain them): one cell lies below every cut of the class and holds the core and 5,280 pieces; the others hold 86 pieces (720 cells), 87 (120), 111 (480) or 385 (120).
- **F4.** For each class, the left factors $\hat n_c u\hat n_c^{-1}$ ($u\in2I$) lie in exactly five left cosets $a\,2I$ of $2I$, one of them $2I$; the other four cosets of the five classes are 20 distinct cosets. (This is the computational side of Theorem A.)

## Theorems to prove

Fix a class $\mathcal P$ and let $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$ be the rotation group of the 600-cell with vertex set $\mathcal P$.

- **A (structure).** $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P(c))=\{[a,b]:a\in\hat n_c\,2I\,\hat n_c^{-1},\ b\in2I\}$; $\Lambda_c$ is the stabiliser of $\hat n_c$ in it; $H_{\mathcal P}=\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)\cap K^+$ has index 5 in $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$. Hence exactly five domains meet $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$: the lattice domain $K^+$ and four others, the *frames of $\mathcal P$*. Different classes share only the lattice domain, so there are 20 non-lattice frames in all. A twist of a grip of $\mathcal P$ takes every piece it moves from the lattice domain or a frame of $\mathcal P$ into the lattice domain or a frame of $\mathcal P$.
- **B (never blocked).** If a piece's domain is a frame of $\mathcal P$ (or the lattice domain), its posed region does not straddle the cut of any grip of $\mathcal P$. Consequently, in a valid configuration whose pieces all lie in the lattice domain or in frames of $\mathcal P$, every grip of $\mathcal P$ is admissible, and every twist of a grip of $\mathcal P$ keeps this property.
- **C (an ordinary puzzle).** Twists of grips of $\mathcal P$ permute the cells of the $\mathcal P$-cut arrangement (as regions of $\mathbb R^4$, independent of frame) and carry the contents of each cell rigidly. Hence the configurations reachable from solved by twists of $\mathcal P$ alone are finitely many, they form one orbit of a finite group $G_{\mathcal P}$ acting on cell contents (a vertex-turning puzzle on the 600-cell $\mathcal P$, cut at depth $\alpha$, whose outline $\widetilde P$ is not $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$-invariant), and such a configuration is on the lattice exactly when every cell content has a pose in $H_{\mathcal P}$.
- **D (restoring one class).** Call a configuration *of depth one* when every domain is the lattice domain or a frame. For a class $\mathcal P$, a fragment of $\mathcal P$ is the set of pieces in one frame of $\mathcal P$.
  1. *Necessary condition.* If a word in twists of $\mathcal P$ puts a fragment $F$ of $\mathcal P$ on the lattice and keeps every lattice piece on the lattice, then every $\mathcal P$-cell that contains a piece of $F$ contains no lattice piece (*$F$ fills whole cells*). Prove this from C.
  2. *Sufficient condition.* Suppose $X$ is of depth one, the fragments of $\mathcal P$ fill whole cells, and a set $A\subseteq\mathcal P$ of grips has caps that contain no piece of any other class's frame and whose cuts no region straddles. State certificates about the group generated by the twists of $A$ acting on cell contents (for example: pure three-cycles of cells and pure changes of one cell content's pose by an element of $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$, each fixing every other cell, together with setups) under which some word in twists of $A$ ends with every piece of the lattice domain and of the frames of $\mathcal P$ on the lattice (positions may change) and moves no piece of another class's frame. Prove it, and say which parity or orientation invariants of $G_{\mathcal P}$ could obstruct it (the stabiliser of a cell in $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$ can be non-trivial; $A_5$ is perfect).
  3. *Classes one after another.* Prove that if the sufficient condition holds for each class in some order, applied to the configuration left by the previous classes, the configuration reaches the lattice.

## Observed (exact computations; leads, not proofs)

- A scramble of 360 twists of the grips of one class (145 jumble, 215 retained) leaves 93.5% of the pieces off the lattice in exactly four domains, the four frames of that class; every fragment there fills whole cells. All 480 grips of the other classes are blocked and none of its own class.
- At a configuration where a set of restoration rules stops (from a 40-twist scramble), all 860 off-lattice pieces are in frames: fragments of 424, 164 and 12 pieces of one class, 197 of a second, 63 of a third. The fragments of 164 and 197 fill whole cells; each of the others shares one cell with lattice pieces (25, 86 and 86 of them). A two-cap commutator of grips of the first class returned the fragment of 164.
- A later stop has fragments of 424 and 12 (first class), 50 and 48 (second), 63 (third) and 93 (fifth); those of 50 and 48 fill whole cells, the other four do not. None of the 22,656 two-cap commutators $(d,a)(c,q)(d,a^{-1})(c,q^{-1})$ of one searched family (cap $d$ holds a fragment $F$ whole, $a$ puts $F$ on the lattice, and $F$ lies in the common part of the caps $c$ and $d$) lowers the off-lattice count there; other words of this form were not searched. Of the 120 grips of each class with fragments, 110 to 114 are admissible and have caps free of other classes' fragments.
- At all three configurations no piece of a frame of a class straddles a cut of that class.

## E (optional)

By D1, no word of twists of its own class puts a fragment that shares a cell with lattice pieces on the lattice while keeping every lattice piece on the lattice (single twists of its class can return it, but they move lattice pieces off the lattice: at the later stop above one twist returns the fragment of 424 pieces and moves 2,933 lattice pieces off). Describe what must happen: which twists of other classes can separate the two parts of such a cell, what domains they create (depth two: a piece of a frame of $\mathcal P$ caught by a jumble twist of a class $\mathcal Q\neq\mathcal P$ gets a pose in $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal Q)\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)K^+$), and whether a bounded number of such twists, followed by D, always suffices. Partial results and counterexample shapes are useful.
