# Problem cards for an external prover

Self-contained problem statements for a natural-language proof assistant (the owner runs it locally). Each card restates the model it needs, lists what is proved and what is only computed, and asks for a proof blueprint that names every machine check as a certificate. A returned blueprint is a lead until the integrator checks it and, where it names certificates, until exact code confirms them.

## Answers so far (26 October 2026)

- **Card 1.** Accepted theorem: the certificates and F1 imply G = 𝒦. All 35 position and 11 frame certificates were then constructed and pass an independent checker, so the retained group equals the kernel of its invariants, without hypothesis C ([restoration stage 03](../../restoration/03-retained-group-and-lattice-restorer/README.md)).
- **Card 2.** Answered by a counterexample: the 9-twist journal C2 reaches a lattice state that breaks the invariants. Card 5 starts from it.
- **Card 3.** Theorems A, B and D1 were accepted; card 5 uses them as given.
- **Card 5.** Five of its seven sub-questions are answered, among them A_J = C2^34 × C5^2 exactly; the normal form from height 6 (1.3) stays open ([restoration stage 04](../../restoration/04-card5-lattice-invariants/README.md)). Finiteness (1.1) was answered on 17 October 2026: R(I_a) is infinite ([restoration stage 10](../../restoration/10-x1-unbounded/README.md)).
- **Card 6** (24 October 2026). Joint attack by Astra and Claude Opus, under the owner's decision of that day. Question 1.1 is answered by a proved construction with finite hypotheses at the state and a length bound (the precedence peel T3P, at most 81 letters per round with clean helpers), which gives a tame descent at the D2 height end of seed 7304; Question 1.2 by an exact safety table and its symmetry; the mechanism of Question 3.2 by a per-cap accounting (each lowering turns the pieces it lowers into the cap's new foreign load, and after a best lowering, one with the largest supply, the cap descends again only after letters at other grips lower that load or raise its lowering supply). Questions 1.3, 2 and 3.1 are answered in part, and 3.3 is open: chained greedily, the construction's rounds lower Φ by 6% to 56% at the six D2 height ends and then jam ([restoration stage 14, section 5](../../restoration/14-blocking-structure/README.md#5-joint-attack-24-october-2026)).
- **Card 7** (24 October 2026). Joint attack by Astra and Claude Opus. Question 1.3 is answered by a chronological certificate for any word (Theorem MC), Question 2.1 by a proved lowering machine (a finite controller on two clean caps that share a high unit, four-letter lens cells and the reverse pump) that has no instance where it was tested, and Question 2.3 as a finite test (re-aims); Questions 1.1, 1.2, 2.2 and 3.1 are answered in part and 3.2 is open. Short retained releases free 15 of the 131 own holders that card 6's peel left. A certified greedy procedure over five families of such macros lowers Φ at all six chain ends and then stops for good at every one of them, where no macro of its families and no single letter lowers Φ, so Question 4 is answered negatively for those families ([restoration stage 15, section 2](../../restoration/15-lowering-machine/README.md#2-joint-attack-24-october-2026)).
- **Card 8** (25 October 2026). Joint attack by Astra and Claude Opus. Every jam of two-level P* has a certified tame descent, so the least excursion is 0 at all six: transports carry supply to a target that is not an own holder (decided at the state, Theorem X), and routing releases take a blocked grip's straddlers through one clean cap while helpers move the obligated pieces inside brackets (Lemmas OT, RR and CT). Questions 1.2, 1.3, 2 and 3 are answered, Question 1.1 for four of the six holders (307 and 517 stay open), and Question 4 in part: with transports at its stops the procedure continues at all six jams and stops again, and where it stops depends on the route ([restoration stage 16, section 2](../../restoration/16-six-jams/README.md#2-joint-attack-25-october-2026)). On 26 October 2026 routing releases entered its stop search (P***): from the nine stops of the procedure it continues at eight and stops again at Φ 386 to 1158, where the blocked grips are released in many configurations but no tail of its forms descends ([restoration stage 17](../../restoration/17-routing-in-driver/README.md)).
- **Card 9** (26 October 2026). Joint attack by Astra and Claude Opus. A whole-cap step lowers the hole count of every orbit of the next state's SAFE-ALL group by exactly its landings there, so a whole-cap plan is a finite test at the state before any word is built (two equivalent forms: a per-stage test on virtual states and a layered integral flow), and a state-only rule (fewest single-cap stages, then lexicographic) finds the rest of its plan at every later state. At the depth 40 tail stop it gives three stages, which were built at the states reached and pass the length and composed gates (their twist-by-twist replays were stopped at the freeze): 688,686 letters against 1,510,756 for the four steps first found. From D1's entry whole caps alone stick, by a finite certificate on a residue of 356 pieces, so the glued unit 112734 has no whole-cap route there (Question 2.2: no) and Cover is not inductive; a rule that completes from the entry is proved only along D1's recorded path. Length bounds are linear in the holes moved and the pieces landed but depend on the seed lengths ([restoration stage 18, section 3](../../restoration/18-tail-completion/README.md#3-joint-attack-26-october-2026)). With this card the jumbling research is frozen.
