# Card 2: do lattice states reached through jumbling keep the retained invariants?

Open problem L2 of the jumbling theory. Answer it with a proof, with a reduction to a finite check that a computer can carry out, or with a precise description of what a counterexample must look like. Partial results are useful; label each statement proved, conditional (name the condition) or conjectured.

## Model

Use the model of the retained 600-cell puzzle with rigid jumbling:

- $\widetilde P\subset\mathbb R^4$ is the 600-cell scaled so that its 600 facet poles $n_c$ are known exactly over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$; each grip $c$ has the cut $H_c=\{x:n_c\cdot x=\alpha\|n_c\|^2\}$ with $\alpha=121/125$. The cuts divide $\operatorname{int}\widetilde P$ into chambers; $177{,}120$ of them are the home regions of the pieces, and the core (below every cut) never moves. $K^+$, the rotation group of the 600-cell (order $7200$), permutes the grips, maps chambers to chambers and preserves the menus below.
- **Menus.** $A4_c$ is the tetrahedral rotation group fixing $n_c$ (the retained cap rotations). A menu is $\Lambda_c=t\Lambda_0t^{-1}$ ($t\in K^+$, $tn_0=n_c$) where $\Lambda_0$ is one of: $S4_0$ (the normaliser of $A4_0$ in the rotations fixing $n_0$), or one of the two icosahedral rotation groups $I_a,I_b$ containing $A4_0$. A retained twist uses an element of $A4_c$; a jumble twist an element of $\Lambda_c\setminus A4_c$.
- **Configurations and twists.** A configuration gives every piece $A$ a pose $X[A]\in SO(4)$, with posed region $X[A](\bar A_0)$; it is valid when the interiors of the posed regions and of the core are pairwise disjoint. A twist $(c,g)$ is admissible when no posed region straddles $H_c$ (has interior points strictly on both sides); it then left-multiplies by $g$ the pose of every piece whose posed region lies in $H_c^+$. Reachable configurations are the results of admissible twist sequences from the solved configuration (all poses the identity); they are valid. A lattice configuration is a valid configuration with every pose in $K^+$. Its posed regions are chambers other than the core and pairwise distinct, so it occupies every such chamber exactly once.
- **Stickers and the retained group.** Every piece carries rigidly attached stickers ($259{,}800$ in all). The retained legal group $G$ is generated by the $1{,}200$ retained generators (one half-turn and one third-turn of $A4_c$ per grip). The chambers other than the core fall into 36 $K^+$-orbits: the 600 centres (the centre of grip $c$ is the chamber that lies in cap $c$ and in no other cap) and 35 moving position orbits $o$ with $N_o$ chambers each. On a reachable lattice configuration $Y$ every centre is in its own slot. (Proof: the centre's posed region always contains the facet centre $n_c$, which lies in no other cap, since $n_c\cdot n_d<\alpha\|n_c\|^2$ for every $d\neq c$, checked exactly; so only twists of grip $c$ move the centre, its pose stays in $\Lambda_c$, and on the lattice it lies in $\Lambda_c\cap K^+=A4_c$, which maps the centre to itself.) Every sticker of $Y$ then sits in a retained sticker slot and every moving orbit is mapped onto itself, so $Y$ defines a labelled state $\pi(Y)\in\mathcal W=\prod_{o}H_o\wr S_{N_o}$ with frame groups $H_o\in\{1,C_2,C_5,D_5,A_5\}$. A valid lattice configuration that is not reachable need not keep the centres: giving every piece the same pose $k\in K^+$, $k\neq1$, moves them. Throughout, $\pi$ is used only on reachable lattice configurations.
- **The invariants.** For each orbit, $\nu_o(h_1,\dots,h_N;\sigma)=(\operatorname{sgn}\sigma,\ \overline{h_1\cdots h_N}\in H_o^{\mathrm{ab}})$. Every element of $G$ has $\nu_o=0$ for every $o$ (Lemma 8 of the retained theory: each $\nu_o$ is a homomorphism, and its value on each of the $1{,}200$ generators was computed exactly to be 0).

## Given (proved; use as facts)

- **S (the shape decides).** Admissibility of $(c,g)$, its inside set as a set of regions, and the set of posed regions after it depend only on the set of posed regions before it. All valid lattice configurations have the same set of posed regions (the chamber tiling). Hence a twist sequence that is admissible from solved and ends on the lattice is admissible from every reachable lattice configuration and acts on all of them by one fixed relabelling. The relabellings of reachable lattice configurations form a finite group $G_J\supseteq G$.
- **Inverse and composition.** If $(c,g)$ is admissible from $X$, then $(c,g^{-1})$ is admissible afterwards with the same inside set and returns $X$. Consecutive twists of one grip compose: $(c,g')$ after $(c,g)$ is $(c,g'g)$.
- **Domains.** Two pieces lie in one rotated copy of the chamber tiling exactly when their poses lie in one left coset $\gamma K^+$; such a class is a domain. A pose lies in $K^+$ exactly when both of its positions on two 5-regular Bruhat–Tits trees (at the prime 2, from the left and right quaternion factors of the pose) are the base vertex. Under $I_a$ every reachable pose has right height 0 and even left height; under $I_b$ the mirror; under $S4_0$ the sum of the heights is even.
- **C (conjugated third-turns).** If $u\,(d,a)\,u^{-1}$ ends on the lattice, with $u$ a twist sequence and $a\in A4_d$ of order 3, its relabelling has $\nu_o=0$ for every $o$ (it is a conjugate of a permutation of order 3, so every cycle has length 1 or 3, and every $H_o^{\mathrm{ab}}$ has order prime to 3).

## Computed facts (exact; leads, not proofs)

- The 16 lens words $(0,q)(d,a)(0,q^{-1})$ (8 for $S4_0$ and 8 for $I_a$; $q$ a jumble element, $a$ a third-turn of an overlapping grip $d$), which end on the lattice without being a retained twist, all have $\nu=0$.
- No reachable lattice configuration found so far has $\nu\neq0$ on any orbit.
- Conjugated half-turns $u\,(d,a)\,u^{-1}$ with $a$ of order 2 are not covered by C: a permutation of order 2 can be odd.

## Questions

1. Prove $\nu_o(\pi(Y))=0$ for every reachable lattice configuration $Y$ and every orbit $o$, or reduce this to a finite set of words whose relabellings a computer can evaluate. One route: show that $G_J$ is generated by $G$ together with the relabellings of a describable family of words (for example conjugates $u(d,a)u^{-1}$), then treat that family.
2. If a full answer is out of reach: prove it for conjugated half-turns, or describe exactly what a counterexample must contain (for example a lattice-returning word in which some piece has a pose outside $K^+$ for an odd number of twists).
3. State any invariant that is defined on all reachable configurations (not only on the lattice), is preserved or changed predictably by every admissible twist, and restricts to $\nu$ on the lattice. If none can exist, say why.
