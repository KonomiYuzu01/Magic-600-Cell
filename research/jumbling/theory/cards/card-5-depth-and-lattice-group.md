# Card 5: depth on the left tree, and the lattice correction group, for the menu I_a

Open problems R1 (deep configurations) and L2 (after its counterexample) of the jumbling theory, for the one menu that is now in scope: the icosahedral menu $I_a$. The menus $S4_0$ and $I_b$ are out of scope. Answer Questions 1 to 3. Each answer may be a proof, a reduction to finite certificates that a computer can check, or a precise counterexample or counterexample shape. Partial results are useful. Label every statement proved, conditional (name the condition) or conjectured. Name every machine check as a certificate with its exact statement, and do not claim that a certificate holds.

## Model

**Quaternions.** Identify $\mathbb R^4$ with $\mathbb H$ (basis $1,i,j,k$). $2I\subset\mathbb H$ is the binary icosahedral group (120 unit icosians). Write $[a,b]$ for the rotation $x\mapsto a\,x\,b^{-1}$; $[a,b]=[-a,-b]$. $K^+=\{[u,v]:u,v\in2I\}$ is the rotation group of the 600-cell with vertex set $2I$ (order 7200).

**Grips, cuts, pieces.** The 600 unit poles $\hat n_c$ point to the facet centres of the 600-cell $2I$; $K^+$ permutes them transitively. The puzzle body $\widetilde P$ is that 600-cell with facets $\{x:n_c\cdot x=\|n_c\|^2\}$, $n_c=\rho\hat n_c$. Grip $c$ has the cut $H_c=\{x:n_c\cdot x=\alpha\|n_c\|^2\}$, $\alpha=121/125$, and the cap $H_c^+$ (the side containing $n_c$). The cuts divide $\operatorname{int}\widetilde P$ into chambers; the core (below every cut) never moves, and each of the other $177{,}120$ chambers is the home region $\bar A_0$ of one piece $A$.

**Classes and the menu.** The 600 poles split into five classes $\mathcal P(c)=\hat n_c\cdot2I$ of 120 poles; each class is the vertex set of a regular 600-cell. The menu at grip $c$ is
$$\Lambda_c=\{[\hat n_c u\hat n_c^{-1},\,u]:u\in2I\},$$
the stabiliser of $\hat n_c$ in $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P(c))$; it has 60 elements, and $A4_c=\Lambda_c\cap K^+$ (12 elements) are the retained twists. The other 48 are jumble twists.

**Configurations and twists.** A configuration $X$ gives every piece $A$ a pose $X[A]\in SO(4)$ and the posed region $X[A](\bar A_0)$; it is valid when the interiors of the posed regions and of the core are pairwise disjoint. A twist $(c,g)$, $g\in\Lambda_c$, is admissible from $X$ when no posed region straddles $H_c$ (has interior points strictly on both sides); it then left-multiplies by $g$ the pose of every piece whose posed region lies in $H_c^+$. Reachable configurations are the results of admissible twist sequences from the solved configuration (every pose the identity). A piece is on the lattice when its pose lies in $K^+$. A domain is a left coset $\gamma K^+$; pieces whose poses lie in one domain lie in one rotated copy of the chamber tiling.

**Lattice relabellings and invariants (as in Card 2).** The chambers other than the core fall into 36 $K^+$-orbits: the 600 centres and 35 moving orbits $o$ with $N_o$ chambers. On a reachable lattice configuration $Y$ every centre is in its own slot, so $Y$ defines a labelled state $\pi(Y)\in\mathcal W=\prod_o H_o\wr S_{N_o}$ with frame groups $H_o\in\{1,C_2,C_5,D_5,A_5\}$. For each orbit, $\nu_o=(\operatorname{sgn}\sigma,\ \overline{h_1\cdots h_N}\in H_o^{\mathrm{ab}})$, and $\nu=(\nu_o)_o$ takes values in the finite abelian group $\mathcal A=\prod_o(\mathbb Z/2\times H_o^{\mathrm{ab}})$. $G$ is the retained group (generated by the 1,200 retained generators); $G_J\supseteq G$ is the group of relabellings of reachable lattice configurations.

## Given (proved; use as facts)

- **T (tree model).** Write a pose as $\gamma=[l,r]$ with $l,r$ quaternions over $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, defined up to scalars. At the prime 2 (inert in $F$) the algebra splits and $PGL_2(F_2)$ acts on a 5-regular Bruhat–Tits tree; $v_0$ is the vertex of the icosian order. The pose has the left position $t_L(\gamma)=[l]v_0$ and the right position $t_R(\gamma)=[r]v_0$, and the heights $d_L=d(v_0,t_L)$, $d_R=d(v_0,t_R)$.
  - **T3.** For every pose in the group generated by the menus: $\gamma\in K^+$ if and only if $d_L=d_R=0$.
  - **T4 (computed).** For every grip $c$, the left factors of $\Lambda_c$ fix a neighbour $w_L(c)$ of $v_0$; the right factors fix $v_0$; every jumble element has heights $(2,0)$.
  - **T5.** A twist $(c,h)$ maps the positions of every piece it moves by $(t_L,t_R)\mapsto(h_Lt_L,h_Rt_R)$. Hence every reachable pose has $d_R=0$ and even $d_L$. A jumble element changes $d_L$ by $-2$ when the geodesic from $v_0$ to $t_L$ runs $v_0,w_L(c),h^{-1}v_0,\dots$; by $0$ when it runs $v_0,w_L(c),y$ with $y\neq h^{-1}v_0$; by $+2$ otherwise ($t_L=v_0$ or another first step). Retained twists change no height.
  - **T6.** Two pieces lie in one domain if and only if they have the same tree positions. At height 2 there are exactly 20 domains.
  - **T7.** A piece at left height $d_L$ must still be moved by at least $d_L/2$ jumble twists before it is on the lattice.
- **Card 3 A, B, D1 (accepted).**
  - **A.** $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)=\{[a,b]:a\in\hat n_c2I\hat n_c^{-1},b\in2I\}$ has order 7200; $H_{\mathcal P}=\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)\cap K^+$ has index 5. $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$ meets the lattice domain and four further domains, the frames of $\mathcal P$; the 20 frames of the five classes are distinct.
  - **B.** A piece in the lattice domain or in a frame of $\mathcal P$ does not straddle the cut of any grip of $\mathcal P$.
  - **D1.** Two pieces in one cell of the class-$\mathcal P$ cut arrangement receive the same accumulated left matrix under every admissible word of class-$\mathcal P$ twists.
  - Correction to Card 3 C: the 1,441 cells inside $\widetilde P$ are not fixed slots under the full menu, because order-5 twists move cells outside $\widetilde P$. The correct model uses the sign cells of the 120 class cuts in all of $\mathbb R^4$.
- **Card 2 (accepted).** L2 is false for $I_a$; see Computed fact C2 below for the word.
- **S (shape decides; Card 2).** A twist sequence that is admissible from solved and ends on the lattice is admissible from every reachable lattice configuration and acts on all of them by one fixed relabelling.
- **Card 1 (accepted, conditional).** If 35 position certificates and 11 frame certificates (pure retained words of stated kinds) exist, then $G=\mathcal K:=\ker\nu$. The words themselves have not been exhibited yet.

## Proved here (short proofs; not independently reviewed)

**Lemma 5.1 (class centres).** For each class $\mathcal P$ there is a unique neighbour $w_{\mathcal P}$ of $v_0$ fixed by the left factor of every element of every $\Lambda_c$, $c\in\mathcal P$. The map $\mathcal P\mapsto w_{\mathcal P}$ is a bijection from the five classes onto the five neighbours of $v_0$.

*Proof.* For $d=\hat n_cu_0\in\mathcal P(c)$ the left factors of $\Lambda_d$ form $\hat n_d2I\hat n_d^{-1}=\hat n_c\,u_02Iu_0^{-1}\hat n_c^{-1}=\hat n_c2I\hat n_c^{-1}=:L_{\mathcal P}$, so all grips of one class have the same left factor group. $L_{\mathcal P}$ fixes $w_L(c)$ (T4) and does not fix $v_0$ (jumble elements have $d_L=2$). The fixed set of a group of tree automorphisms is a subtree, so $L_{\mathcal P}$ fixes no second neighbour of $v_0$ (else it would fix $v_0$ between them); hence $w_{\mathcal P}:=w_L(c)$ is unique and depends only on the class. A jumble twist of class $\mathcal P$ sends a lattice piece to $h_Lv_0$, a neighbour of $h_Lw_{\mathcal P}=w_{\mathcal P}$; so all frames of $\mathcal P$ sit at the 4 vertices at distance 2 behind $w_{\mathcal P}$. If $w_{\mathcal P}=w_{\mathcal Q}$ for $\mathcal P\neq\mathcal Q$, their 8 distinct frames (Card 3 A) would be domains at 4 positions, contradicting T6. $\square$

For a piece at $t_L\neq v_0$, its **outer class** is the class $\mathcal P$ with $w_{\mathcal P}$ on the geodesic from $v_0$ to $t_L$.

**Lemma 5.2 (who can lower a piece).** Let $(d,h)$ be a jumble twist with $d$ in class $\mathcal Q$, and let it move a piece at left position $t$. Then $d_L$ falls by 2 exactly when the outer class of $t$ is $\mathcal Q$ and the second vertex of the geodesic is $h^{-1}v_0$; it is unchanged when the outer class is $\mathcal Q$ and the second vertex is another one; it rises by 2 otherwise, in particular for every lattice piece and every piece of another outer class. (T5 with Lemma 5.1.)

**Lemma 5.3 (class invariance).** For each class $\mathcal Q$, every twist of every grip of $\mathcal Q$ (retained or jumble) leaves $d(w_{\mathcal Q},t_L)$ unchanged for every piece. Hence a word in twists of $\mathcal Q$ alone can put a piece on the lattice only if $d(w_{\mathcal Q},t_L)=1$, that is, only if the piece is on the lattice or in a frame of $\mathcal Q$.

*Proof.* The left factors lie in $L_{\mathcal Q}$, which fixes $w_{\mathcal Q}$, and tree automorphisms are isometries. $\square$

**Lemma 5.4 (finiteness).** $R(I_a)$ is finite if and only if the left heights of reachable poses are bounded.

*Proof.* If $d_L\le2m$ on every reachable pose, the reachable domains are among the finitely many vertices within distance $2m$ of $v_0$ (T6), each domain holds 7200 poses, so finitely many poses and finitely many configurations occur. Conversely finitely many configurations have finitely many poses. $\square$

- **C2 (the correction word).** The Card 2 counterexample for $I_a$ is the 9-twist word $w_9=u\,(16,a)\,u^{-1}$, where $u$ is four 72° jumble twists at grips 0, 2, 161 and 139 (exact matrices in the attached journal) and $a$ is the half-turn of $A4_{16}$ with generator word $[33]$. The position permutation is odd on exactly two moving orbits, $o_8$ and $o_{25}$ (3,600 chambers each, 17 two-cycles each), and even on the other 33. - **C3 (other conjugates).** With $u$ fixed as in C2, every half-turn $a$ of $A4_d$ was tried at every grip $d$ within 55.7° of grips 0, 2, 161 and 139 (203 grips), and every non-identity element at grip 16. ## Questions

### 1. Is $R(I_a)$ finite?

By Lemma 5.4 this asks whether reachable left heights are bounded.

1. Prove a bound $d_L\le2m$ for every reachable configuration, with $m$ explicit, or construct an explicit family of words admissible from solved along which the largest height grows without bound, each twist certified admissible.
2. Whatever the answer, describe the mechanism. By Lemma 5.2, a piece rises only when a jumble twist of a class other than its outer class catches it whole. For a domain $\gamma K^+$ let $\operatorname{Safe}(\gamma)=\{d:\gamma^{-1}\hat n_d\text{ is a pole}\}$; no piece of that domain straddles the cut of a grip in $\operatorname{Safe}(\gamma)$, since chambers never straddle cuts. Card 3 B says $\operatorname{Safe}(\gamma)\supseteq\mathcal P$ for a frame of $\mathcal P$. Determine $\operatorname{Safe}$ for the height-4 domains (or bound it) and say what it implies for the next rise.
3. Prove or refute the normal form: the reachable left positions at height $2m$ are exactly the vertices whose geodesic from $v_0$ visits class centres in a sequence of classes $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2,\dots,\mathcal P_m$ with $\mathcal P_i\neq\mathcal P_{i+1}$, and the corresponding domains are $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P_1)\cdots\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P_m)K^+$ minus the shorter products. (At height 2 this is Card 3 A; the tree has $5\cdot4^{2m-1}$ vertices at distance $2m$.)

### 2. Depth reduction read from the current state

Reversing the journal always lowers every height to 0, but it uses the history. The goal is a rule that reads only the current configuration $X$ (its poses and the fixed model).

1. Give a construction that takes every reachable configuration $X$ to a depth-one configuration (every $d_L\le2$), with a progress measure that strictly decreases at every step, such as $\Phi(X)=\sum_p\max(0,d_L(p)-2)$ or the lexicographic pair (largest height, number of pieces at that height). Every step must be a finite exact test on $X$ plus an admissible word. Lattice pieces may leave the lattice on the way; only depth matters here.
2. If a single-twist step cannot always exist, say why. By Lemma 5.2 a lowering twist of class $\mathcal Q$ raises every lattice piece and every piece of another outer class inside its cap. Give the conditions under which a conjugate $S\,(d,h)\,S^{-1}$ or a commutator returns those pieces while the target fragment keeps its net lowering. As in the lens commutator analysis, the pieces to watch are those caught by a twist but not by its inverse, and those caught by every twist of the word.
3. If no current-only rule of this kind can exist, give a reachable configuration (exact word from solved) that defeats it, and the invariant or obstruction that makes it fail.

The output of this question only has to be some depth-one configuration. Matching it to the depth-one families already solved under Card 3 E is a separate obligation; do not assume it.

### 3. The lattice correction group of $I_a$

Since $G\subseteq G_J$ and $G=\ker\nu$ under the Card 1 certificates, $G_J=\nu^{-1}(\mathcal A_J)$ with $\mathcal A_J:=\nu(G_J)\le\mathcal A$, and $G_J/G\cong\mathcal A_J$. By S, every lattice-returning word is a correction macro that acts the same on every reachable lattice configuration.

1. Determine $\mathcal A_J$. Give explicit lattice-returning words whose $\nu$ generate it (lower bound), and prove that nothing else occurs (upper bound). For the upper bound, a natural candidate is a quantity defined on all reachable configurations with an explicit per-twist update whose restriction to the lattice is $\nu$ modulo $\mathcal A_J$. Card 2 reports that the exact failure in its counterexample is the change of a cap count modulo 2 on the relevant orbit; make that precise for every twist.
2. Is the sign of $o_8$ always equal to the sign of $o_{25}$ on $G_J$? If so, explain the pairing; if not, give a word that separates them.
3. Is every frame component of $\nu$ zero on $G_J$?
4. Are conjugated half-turns $u\,(d,a)\,u^{-1}$ enough to generate $\mathcal A_J$? Card 2 C already shows that conjugated third-turns give $\nu=0$.
