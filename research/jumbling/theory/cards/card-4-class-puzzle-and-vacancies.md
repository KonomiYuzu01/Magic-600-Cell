# Card 4: the class puzzle at work, and cells shared with the lattice

Open problem R1 of the jumbling theory (restoring deep configurations), continuing card 3. This card stands alone: it restates the model. Part I asks for proofs of the structure that an exact computation used to return two fragments of one class (Theorems G to K). Part II asks the open question of card 3, E, in a sharper form, built on a new observation (vacant chambers). Label every statement proved, conditional (name the condition) or conjectured, and name every machine check as a certificate with its exact statement; do not claim that a certificate holds.

## Model

**Quaternions.** Identify $\mathbb R^4$ with $\mathbb H$. $2I\subset\mathbb H$ is the binary icosahedral group (120 unit quaternions: $\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k$, $\tfrac12(\pm1\pm i\pm j\pm k)$ and the even coordinate permutations of $\tfrac12(\pm\varphi,\pm1,\pm\varphi^{-1},0)$, $\varphi=\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}2$). $[a,b]$ is the rotation $x\mapsto a\,x\,b^{-1}$ ($a,b$ unit quaternions). $K^+=\{[u,v]:u,v\in2I\}$ (order 7200).

**Grips, outline, cuts.** The 600 poles $\hat n_c$ point to the facet centres of the 600-cell $2I$. The outline $\widetilde P$ is the 600-cell $\{x:n_c\cdot x\le\|n_c\|^2\ \forall c\}$, $n_c=\rho\,\hat n_c$; grip $c$ has the cut $H_c=\{n_c\cdot x=\alpha\|n_c\|^2\}$, $\alpha=121/125$, and the cap $H_c^+=\{n_c\cdot x\ge\alpha\|n_c\|^2\}$.

**Pieces and twists.** The cuts divide $\operatorname{int}\widetilde P$ into the fixed core and $177{,}120$ chambers, the home regions of the pieces. A configuration gives each piece a pose in $SO(4)$; it is valid when the posed regions and the core have disjoint interiors. A twist $(c,g)$, $g\in\Lambda_c$, is admissible when no posed region straddles $H_c$; it left-multiplies by $g$ the pose of every piece inside $H_c^+$. Reachable configurations come from the solved one by admissible twists. A piece is on the lattice when its pose lies in $K^+$ (its region is then a chamber); the pieces with poses in one left coset $\gamma K^+$ form a domain. A chamber is *vacant* when no lattice piece occupies it; distinct lattice pieces occupy distinct chambers, so the number of vacant chambers equals the number of pieces off the lattice.

**Menu.** $\Lambda_c=\{[\hat n_c u\hat n_c^{-1},u]:u\in2I\}$ (order 60, fixes $\hat n_c$). Its *retained* part $\Lambda_c\cap K^+$ is a tetrahedral group $A4_c$ (order 12); the other 48 elements are jumble elements.

**Classes, frames, labels (card 3, Theorems A to C; use as given).** The poles split into five classes $\mathcal P(c)=\hat n_c\cdot 2I$ of 120, each the vertex set of a 600-cell; $K^+$ permutes the classes as $A_5$. For a class $\mathcal P$, $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$ is the rotation group of its 600-cell, $\Lambda_c\subset\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P(c))$, and $H_{\mathcal P}=\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)\cap K^+$ has index 5. The *label* of a piece with pose $hk$ ($h\in\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$, $k\in K^+$) is the coset $hH_{\mathcal P}$: five labels, label 0 is $H_{\mathcal P}$ (the lattice), the other four are the *frames of $\mathcal P$*. A twist with element $g\in\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$ maps label $\ell$ to $g\ell$. The 20 frames of the five classes are distinct domains. Pieces on the lattice or in frames of $\mathcal P$ never straddle a cut of $\mathcal P$ (B). The cuts of $\mathcal P$ divide space into *cells of $\mathcal P$*, named by the set of caps of $\mathcal P$ containing them; a twist of a grip of $\mathcal P$ carries the contents of each cell in its cap rigidly to another cell (C). A *fragment* of $\mathcal P$ is the set of pieces in one frame of $\mathcal P$. D1: a word of twists of $\mathcal P$ cannot put a fragment on the lattice while keeping on the lattice the lattice pieces that share a cell of $\mathcal P$ with it.

**Class puzzle.** Fix $\mathcal P$. A *point* is a pair (cell, label). A twist $(c,g)$ of a grip of $\mathcal P$ maps every point $(f,\ell)$ with $f$ in cap $c$ to $(gf,g\ell)$, where $g$ acts on cells through its permutation of $\mathcal P$, and fixes the other points. A *content* is the set of pieces of the lattice and of frames of $\mathcal P$ in one cell of $\mathcal P$. A content is *pure* when its pieces share one label $\ell$; it then sits at the point $(f,\ell)$ of its cell $f$. A content of a cell shared by a fragment and lattice pieces carries two labels, 0 and the fragment's; a twist maps both by the same $g$, so they move together (D1). A grip is *free* when it is admissible and its cap holds no piece of another class's frame.

## The configuration $X_E$

$X_E$ is reachable: a 40-twist scramble, then 86 twists of two restoration attempts. 690 pieces are off the lattice in six fragments: 424 and 12 (class 0), 50 and 48 (class 1), 63 (class 2), 93 (class 4); 690 chambers are vacant. Pieces of a frame of $\mathcal P$ never straddle a cut of $\mathcal P$ here, as B requires.

## Part I: theorems to prove

- **G (cells and stars).** Prove G1 for every class: the cells of $\mathcal P$ that meet $\bigcup_{h\in\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)}h(\widetilde P)$ are the cell below every cut and the faces of the 600-cell on $\mathcal P$, so a cap holds exactly the closed star of its pole. Then prove G3, with the chamber counts.
- **H (label orbits are invariants).** For a face $f$ let $S_f\subset\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$ be its stabiliser. Show that the label action of $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$ on the five cosets has kernel $\{[\pm1,b]:b\in2I\}$ and that this kernel meets every $S_f$ trivially; identify $S_f$ for vertices, edges, triangles and tetrahedra (orders 60, 10, 6, 12) and their orbits on the five labels (5, 5, 3 + 2, 4 + 1). Every twist of $\mathcal P$ preserves the $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$-orbit of a content's point, so a content reaches label 0 only at cells $f$ with $(f,0)$ in its orbit. Twist orbits can be smaller than $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$-orbits (vertex cells never move). State the certificate under which G2 holds: at a free vertex cell the twist group is transitive on the five labels; on edge, triangle and tetrahedron cells with a free vertex its orbits are the $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$-orbits of points restricted to those cells.
- **I (counting).** Contents never merge or split under twists of $\mathcal P$. Suppose every own content is pure. Prove: all own contents can be put on label 0 by twists of $\mathcal P$ only if, for every orbit $O$ of points, the number of contents in $O$ is at most the number of cells $f$ with $(f,0)\in O$. Give certificates on the twist group (for example, 3-cycles of contents within an orbit with all other points fixed) under which this count also suffices, and apply them to G4 (480 contents for 480 cells).
- **J (no conservation law on edge cells).** A *pure edge twist* is an element of the twist group that fixes every point outside edge cells and maps each edge cell to itself. Show that it acts on each edge cell $e$ by an element of $S_e\cong D_5$. Explain G5's single ratio per pair (a transport between the rotation groups of two edge cells), show that the product of the ratios around a triangle of edges does not depend on the chosen 5-cycles, and prove or refute: with all 120 grips free, every edge cell can be twisted alone, so no $\mathbb Z_5$-valued sum over edge cells is conserved (unlike the edge-flip sum of the Rubik's cube). State the certificate that would decide it.
- **K (one class, whole cells).** Combining G to J: if every fragment of $\mathcal P$ fills whole cells (shares no cell with lattice pieces, so every own content is pure), the counting condition of I holds, and certificates of I and J hold for the free grips, then a word of free twists of $\mathcal P$ puts every fragment of $\mathcal P$ on the lattice and leaves every lattice piece on the lattice and every other domain unchanged.

## Part II: shared cells (the open question)

Lemma to prove first. **L.** For a grip $d$ of class $\mathcal Q\neq\mathcal P$, $\Lambda_d\cap\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)$ has order 3 and lies in $K^+$ (hint: a jumble element in $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)\cap\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal Q)$ would make its domain a frame of both classes). A retained twist $(d,k)$ keeps every lattice piece on the lattice, permutes the chambers of its cap (so it moves vacant chambers as blanks) and moves a piece of a frame of $\mathcal P$ into a frame of $k(\mathcal P)$.

M2 and M3 suggest this picture: in a shared cell the lattice part sits in chambers inside $\widetilde P$ and the fragment part sticks out of $\widetilde P$, and twists of the fragment's own class move the two parts together (D1). A cut of another class that separates them is useless while its cap holds no vacant chamber, because a retained twist of it only permutes occupied chambers (M3). The cell becomes the fragment's alone once its chambers are all vacant. So the question is about moving vacant chambers.

- **E1.** Prove the picture: in a reachable configuration whose domains are the lattice and frames, (a) a cell of $\mathcal P$ holds no lattice piece exactly when all its chambers are vacant; (b) a retained twist of a grip whose cap holds no vacant chamber leaves the set of vacant chambers unchanged, so it turns no shared cell into an unshared one. Is M1 (no frame piece inside $\widetilde P$) true for every such configuration, or only at $X_E$? If frame pieces can lie inside $\widetilde P$, what replaces the picture?
- **E2 (main).** Prove or refute: from such a configuration, a word of admissible twists exists that ends with every piece that started on the lattice still on it (positions may change), every domain the lattice or a frame, and every fragment (the pieces of one frame, at the end) filling whole cells of its class. Natural tools are retained twists, which move vacant chambers like blanks of an ordinary puzzle (the retained group acting on chambers), conjugated so that they avoid the frame pieces and the blocked cuts; and twists of $\mathcal P$, which move whole cells including their vacant chambers (G4 used an empty cell this way). Give the construction with certificates (for example, a 3-cycle of chambers by retained twists that fixes every frame piece, and its conjugates), or the invariant that obstructs it. The four cases of M2 differ: two cells already have 51 and 61 vacant chambers, two have none.
- **E3.** Is the length of such a word bounded independently of the configuration? A family of configurations that needs ever longer words would be as useful as a bound.
- **E4 (fallback, card 3, E).** If E2 fails, describe the least use of jumble twists of other classes (depth two: a piece of a frame of $\mathcal P$ caught by a jumble twist of $\mathcal Q$ gets a pose in $\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal Q)\operatorname{Sym}^+(\mathcal P)K^+$) that, followed by K, returns a shared fragment.
