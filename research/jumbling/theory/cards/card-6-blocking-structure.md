# Card 6: the blocking structure of deep states, for the menu I_a

Open problem M1 of the restoration (a construction from the state with a bound), in the form that stops the restoration of deep scrambles today: at reachable configurations thousands of twists from solved, the grips that a height-lowering needs are blocked, and the height phase stops with pieces at left height 4. Answer Questions 1 to 3. Each answer may be a proof, a reduction to finite certificates that a computer can check at the state, or a precise counterexample or counterexample shape. Partial results are useful. Label every statement proved, conditional (name the condition) or conjectured. Name every machine check as a certificate with its exact statement, and do not claim that a certificate holds.

The standard here is **tame**, not strict: pieces may leave the lattice, and pieces of height 2 may move anywhere; only left heights matter. The target of this card is a configuration with every left height at most 2 (depth one), which the rest of the restoration takes as input.

## Model

As in [card 5](card-5-depth-and-lattice-group.md), whose model and notation this card uses:

- **Puzzle.** $K^+=\{[u,v]:u,v\in2I\}$ ($[a,b]:x\mapsto axb^{-1}$, order 7200). 600 grips $c$ with unit poles $\hat n_c$, in five classes $\mathcal P(c)=\hat n_c2I$ of 120 (each the vertex set of a regular 600-cell); $\operatorname{CLS}(c)$ is the class of grip $c$. The cut $H_c=\{x:n_c\cdot x=\alpha\|n_c\|^2\}$, $\alpha=121/125$, bounds the cap $H_c^+$. The menu $\Lambda_c=\{[\hat n_cu\hat n_c^{-1},u]:u\in2I\}$ has 60 elements: the 12 retained ones $A4_c=\Lambda_c\cap K^+$ and 48 jumbles. 177,120 pieces, each with a home chamber; a configuration gives each piece $A$ a pose $X[A]$; a twist $(c,g)$ is admissible when no posed region straddles $H_c$, and then left-multiplies by $g$ the pose of every piece whose region lies in $H_c^+$.
- **Tree.** The pose $[l,r]$ of a reachable piece has right position $v_0$ and left position $t(A)=[l]v_0$ on the 5-regular tree; its **height** $h(A)=d(v_0,t(A))$ is even, and $h=0$ exactly on the lattice. The five neighbours of $v_0$ are the class centres $w_{\mathcal P}$ (Lemma 5.1). For $h(A)\ge2$ the **branch** $b(A)$ is the class $\mathcal P$ with $w_{\mathcal P}$ on the geodesic from $v_0$ to $t(A)$, and the **second vertex** $s(A)$ is the vertex of that geodesic at distance 2. Pieces have the same tree position exactly when their poses lie in one domain $\gamma K^+$ (T6); the 20 domains at height 2 are the **frames**, four per class, at the four vertices at distance 2 behind $w_{\mathcal P}$; there are 320 positions at height 4, all reached by two jumbles.
- **Terms of this card.** At a configuration $X$:
  - the label $\ell_X(A,d)\in\{\text{in},\text{out},\text{straddle}\}$ of piece $A$ at grip $d$ says whether its posed region lies in $H_d^+$, outside it, or across $H_d$; $S_d$ is the set of straddlers of $d$ and $I_d$ the set of pieces in the cap;
  - grip $d$ is **clean** when $S_d=\emptyset$ (all its twists are admissible), **blocked** otherwise;
  - the **holders** of a set of pieces are the grips whose caps hold every piece of the set;
  - the **depth** is $\Phi(X)=\sum_A\max(0,h(A)-2)$;
  - the **foreign load** $F_x$ of grip $x$ is the number of pieces in $I_x$ of height at least 2 and branch other than $\operatorname{CLS}(x)$; a clean grip with $F_x=0$ is **pure**;
  - a **unit** is a set of pieces with one pose (so one tree position); a **high unit** has height at least 4; its **own holders** are its holders of class $b(\text{unit})$;
  - a word is **tame** when every letter keeps $\Phi$ from rising (each prefix has $\Phi\le\Phi(X)$).

## Given (proved; use as facts)

- **T5, Lemma 5.2, Lemma 5.3** (card 5). A twist $(c,h)$ maps the left positions of the pieces it moves by $h_L$. A jumble of class $\mathcal Q$ lowers a piece by 2 exactly when its branch is $\mathcal Q$ and its second vertex is $h_L^{-1}v_0$, keeps the height of the other pieces of branch $\mathcal Q$, and raises every other piece by 2, every lattice piece included. Retained twists change no height. A word of one class changes a height by at most 2 in total.
- **Lowering coset** (R21F-03). If a unit at height $h\ge2$ lies whole in a cap of its own branch, exactly 12 of the 60 items there lower it by 2, a left coset of $A4$; for $h\ge4$ three of the twelve send it into each of the other four branches. At a grip of another class the 12 retained items keep its height and the 48 jumbles raise it by 2.
- **Frames by height** (T20-02; card 3 B). For a domain $\gamma K^+$ the cuts $d$ with $\gamma^{-1}\hat n_d$ a pole are all 600 at height 0, exactly the 120 cuts of the branch at height 2, and none at height 4 or more. A piece never straddles such a cut. So a piece of height 2 and branch $\mathcal P$ never blocks a grip of $\mathcal P$, a lattice piece blocks nothing, and a high piece can block any grip.
- **Theory draft, Propositions 1.2, 2.2 and 2.4.** The inverse of an admissible twist is admissible and moves the same set. After an admissible $(c,g)$ every $(c,g')$ is admissible and they compose to $(c,g'g)$. Grip $d$ is blocked exactly when some unit with pose $\gamma$ has $\gamma^{-1}\hat n_d$ not a pole and the hyperplane $\gamma^{-1}H_d$ meets the interior of one of its home chambers.
- **Existence without bounds** (M1-01, Theorem M\*). Every reachable configuration off the lattice has a clean step (a word after which a unit lands and no lattice piece leaves); the inverse of the history is one. R(I_a) is infinite (stage 10), so no uniform bound on such steps exists (T15-04), and a computable bound in the off count and the largest height alone exists exactly when reachability is decidable (Theorem D). Bounds must use more of the state, through hypotheses.
- **Earlier conditional constructions** (senior review rounds 21 and 22; all strict, with existence bounds like $7200^n$): retained conjugation (R22D-01: after a retained letter $(c,e)$ a piece inside $c$ has at grip $x$ the label it had at $e^{-1}(x)$); the lens lemma (R21F-02: four-letter commutators at two clean grips, with cap tests as hypotheses); unlocking by landing the straddlers one at a time with strict steps (R22D-07, Lemma C); ladders and vacating words (R21-03, R21-04, line (b) of round 22). At the deep states below their hypotheses fail at the first level.

## Proved here (short proofs in the [stage 14 page](../../restoration/14-blocking-structure/README.md); not independently reviewed)

**Lemma L (label calculus).** Let $c$ be clean, $k\in A4_c$, $X'=(c,k)X$. A piece outside $I_c$ keeps every label. A piece in $I_c$ has $\ell_{X'}(A,d)=\ell_X(A,k^{-1}d)$ for every grip $d$ (R22D-01), keeps its height, and gets the branch $\sigma_k(b(A))$, where $\sigma_k$ is the permutation of the five classes with $\operatorname{CLS}(k\cdot e)=\sigma_k(\operatorname{CLS}(e))$. So retained twists at clean grips act on the label table by permuting the entries of the moved rows, exactly and without geometry.

**Corollary L1.** Under retained twists at clean grips every piece keeps its straddle number $|\{d:A\in S_d\}|$ and its number of holders; the straddle incidence $\sum_d|S_d|$ is constant, and $|S_d|$ becomes $|S_d|-|I_c\cap S_d|+|I_c\cap S_{k^{-1}d}|$; pieces with equal holder sets keep equal holder sets, and their branches stay equal or stay different.

**Lemma Φ.** For a clean grip $x$ of class $\mathcal Q$ and a jumble $h\in\Lambda_x$: $\Delta\Phi=2(F_x-D_x(h))$, where $D_x(h)$ counts the pieces in $I_x$ of branch $\mathcal Q$, height at least 4 and second vertex $h_L^{-1}v_0$. The lattice pieces of the cap go to height 2 and do not change $\Phi$. So every twist at a pure grip is tame.

**Lemma C (co-holding).** A twist at $c$ moves exactly the pieces of which $c$ is a holder. If a high unit $G$ and a piece $p$ of another branch at height at least 2 have equal holder sets, then $p$ lies in the foreign load of every own holder of $G$, the two stay co-held under retained twists, and a lowering of $G$ after retained twists only raises $p$ in the same letter. Only a jumble can separate them.

**Lemma T (the last jumble).** If $X=(c,h)Y$ with $h$ a jumble and $\operatorname{CLS}(c)=\mathcal Q$, then $c$ is clean at $X$, and $I_c$ holds only pieces of branch $\mathcal Q$, lattice pieces, and the pieces that $h$ lowered from a height $h'\ge4$, whose new branch is not $\mathcal Q$. So $c$ is pure unless the last letter lowered a high piece.

## Questions

### 1. Tame unlocking

1. Give a construction that, from a reachable $X$, a high unit $G$ and an own holder $x$ of $G$, produces a tame word after which some own holder of $G$ is clean, holds $G$, and has $F<D(h)$ for a lowering item $h$ (so that one more letter lowers $\Phi$), with hypotheses that are finite exact tests at $X$ and an explicit length bound in terms of the data of the hypotheses. Allowed letters: retained twists at clean grips (Lemma L) and jumbles with $\Delta\Phi\le0$ (Lemma Φ), among them the landings of obstruction units at clean pure grips of their own class.
2. Landings create new obstruction (C5): a jumble at a pure grip $y$ of class $\mathcal P$ takes the lattice pieces of $I_y$ into frames of $\mathcal P$, which may straddle $H_x$ or lie in $H_x^+$. State and prove the condition under which a landing at $y$ is **safe for $x$** (it lands the unit and adds nothing to $S_x$ or to the foreign load of $x$), as a finite table that depends on the relative position of $y$ and $x$, the item, and which chambers of $I_y$ are occupied by lattice pieces. Say whether the table depends only on the $K^+$-orbit of the pair $(y,x)$ and the item's coset, as the evacuation table R21F-09 does at the solved state.
3. Define the **dependency structure** of $x$ at $X$: the obstruction units, which grips can move each of them tamely (clean pure own-class grips, other clean grips by retained letters, blocked grips), which grips block those, and so on. Prove which of its properties suffice for 1.1 (for example a well-founded peeling order whose landings are safe), and which are obstructions to every tame word: invariants or monovariants of tame letters that rule out a clean own holder with $F<D(h)$. Corollary L1 gives invariants of retained letters; find what survives the tame jumbles.
4. Check your hypotheses against C3 to C5: which own holders at the 7304 stop satisfy them, and what the construction does at 522, 64 and 229.

### 2. Bounded excursions

If tame words cannot always unlock, give a construction with a bounded excursion: a word along which $\Phi\le\Phi(X)+E$ and which ends with $\Phi$ lower, with hypotheses as in 1.1 and explicit bounds on $E$ and on the length. Candidates: raise an obstruction unit through a clean grip of another class, lower $G$, then lower the raised unit at a grip of its new branch; or a lens or commutator around the lowering letter (R21F-02), with the pieces to watch named as in card 5, question 2.2. Use C3: at 2 and 183 the obstruction has no clean holder at all, so any unlocking there starts at grips that block other grips.

### 3. Where jams come from, and an order that does not jam

1. Extend Lemma T from the last letter to a description of the clean and the pure grips of a deep reachable configuration, in terms the state shows. Is there a state-only notion of a **top layer** (units and grips that can be landed or lowered without raising anything) and a theorem that it is non-empty whenever $\Phi>0$, or a reachable configuration where it is empty?
2. Explain C2. By Lemma T a lowering at a pure own holder puts the lowered pieces into another branch inside that cap, so the cap becomes impure; and a census or bulk step that lands pieces leaves other frames behind. Give the mechanism by which the clean pure own holders run out, and say whether an order or a choice among the three lowering items per target branch (Lowering coset) keeps a supply of them.
3. Give a state-only order for peeling and lowering, with a progress measure that every step lowers, that provably does not jam on a stated class of configurations that contains the six D2 ends or explains why they lie outside it; or show that every order of a stated kind jams at some reachable configuration, with the configuration given as an exact word from solved or a pose table.
