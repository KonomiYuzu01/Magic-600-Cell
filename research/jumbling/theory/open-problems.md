### R1 in detail

What is settled:
- Theorem U (proved): every reachable configuration off the lattice has an N-lowering word. Restoration is always possible in principle; the question is a short, findable word.
- Up to 20 twists the rules reach the lattice on every tested input: 51 fixture and random runs and 80 lens words (restoration section 5).
- On 40 and 60 records the repair word passes every earlier stopping point, but the descent slows as N falls (restoration section 3, "Result on deep inputs").

The I_a@40 stop (N = 1,192, grip N = 1,057, 15 misaligned caps):
- No single twist lowers any N_c.
- Each rule of section 3 was run on its own from this configuration. Reopen, conjugate repair, lock and shift each found no lowering step (shift: 33 minutes, cloud timing of research code).
- 135 of the misaligned cells lie on rotated cut hyperplanes that are no grip's cut (off-grip seams). They form four groups, by domain, of 59, 37, 25 and 14 cells.
- The pieces of each group lie in the common part of about six caps, and about half of those caps have a closed cut. For example, the group of 59 lies in caps 30, 34 and 145 (misaligned) and 31, 73 and 89 (closed).
- By M2 a twist changes N only on its own cut. An off-grip seam therefore has to be carried onto a grip's cut before any twist can close it.
- Every repair rule of section 3 opens with a misaligned cap. None opens a closed cap that holds an off-grip seam.
- Of the caps that hold off-grip seams, several are blocked (no admissible twist), among them 31, 73, 96, 152, 153, 295 and 302. Others are free and aligned, among them 64, 89, 183, 283, 290, 301 and 319.

Experiment (scratch script, not kept; exact N): the words (c, x)(d, y) and (c, x)(d, y)(c, z), where c is any of the 20 caps that hold off-grip seams (aligned caps allowed), x any admissible twist of c, d a misaligned cap meeting c, y the twist of d that lowers N_d most and z the twist of c that lowers N_c most. Result: no word lowers N. After almost every opening (c, x), no neighbouring cap has a twist that lowers its own N_d (one candidate in all 20 caps, and it did not lower N).

This excludes only that family. Like the conjugate repair, it tries only the middle twist that lowers N_d most, so words whose middle twist leaves N_d equal or raises it were not tried.

Floor (joint attack; checked against the counts of every misaligned cap at this stop): N_c ≥ |In_c − Out_c|, where In_c and Out_c count the cut cells of c covered inside and outside, and twists of c alone keep both counts. At this stop eight caps are at their floor (17, 34, 50, 162, 187, 237, 315, 390), so no twist of those caps alone can lower N_c; another cap must first change their counts.

### Joint attack on the I_a@40 stop (10 and 11 October 2026)

Two sealed analyses of the same packet (the senior reviewer, and the solver), then a cross-examination of the solver's claims by the senior reviewer, then experiments. Call records and dispositions are private (`work/reviews/`); this section keeps the results. "Diagnosis" marks a result that reads the scramble journal; no rule reads it.

What the stop is (cross-examination verified the first three items):
- **Fragments.** 860 pieces are off the lattice, in 5 domains of 424, 63, 197, 164 and 12 pieces, all at tree height (2, 0); the other 176,260 are on the lattice. N = 1,192 = 535 cells on the surface of the lattice domain (holes) + 657 cells on the surface of the fragments. No cap holds a whole off-lattice domain that makes up most of its inside (a carrier); every fragment is enclosed by the lattice domain.
- **The wall.** At this stop every jumble twist raises its own N_c by at least 1,148 (cap 30), and by 1,322 to 1,566 for a cap whose inside is all lattice; a retained twist changes N_c by 0 to +222. So the first jumble twist of any word costs about N itself.
- **Isolation** (`isolate.py`, exact; `results/isolate-I_a-40-stop.txt`). Only the fragment of 164 pieces is exactly the common part of two caps (30 and 145). For the other four, the intersection of every admissible cap that holds the fragment whole (blocked caps are not counted) still contains 165 to 798 other pieces.
- **Why every rule stops.** Each rule accepts only words with a lowering sub-step (conj, lock, shift, reopen), and no single twist lowers any N_c. Whether a later sub-step could lower N after the wall was left open by the cross-examination.
- **How it arose** (diagnosis). Of the first 31 solver twists, 29 undo one scramble record each, one cancels two and one leaves a retained residue; at that point (S31, N = 8,036) the reverse of the 8 remaining records reaches the lattice, its first twist raising N by 948. The rules instead made repair words that split carriers into fragments. A lowering word from the stop exists (Theorem U): the reverse of the solver's 27 non-identity repair twists and then the 8 undos, 35 twists, with N up to 8,984 on the way and never below 1,192 before the end.

Experiments:
- **Floor and complete ABA.** Every word (c, x)(d, y)(c or d, z) on the cap pairs (315, 154) and (162, 237), with x and y any non-identity twist and z the best final twist: 3,481 admissible prefixes per pair, none lowers N; the floor rules out every prefix.
- **E1: return carriers first** (`e1_carrier.py`) (a single twist that puts a whole carrier back on the lattice, taken even if N rises; words of retained twists only are rejected). From S31: N 8,036 → 504 in 17 twists, then stops with 335 pieces off the lattice, in fragments again. From scratch on S4@40 (`e1_carrier.py`): stuck after 87 twists (5.4 hours) at N = 5,666 with 3,754 pieces off the lattice, from N = 46,394 and 80,455 (the seam restorer on the same input stops at N = 6,748, `results/seams-fixture-S4-40-continued.json`).
- **Lens commutators.** The word (d, a)(c, q)(d, a⁻¹)(c, q⁻¹), where cap d holds a fragment F whole, a·γ_F is in K⁺ and F lies in the common part of c and d. At this stop (30, 44)(145, 39)(30, 15)(145, 33) lowers N from 1,192 to 1,140 and the off-lattice count from 860 to 789: the first lowering word found at this stop that does not come from the journal. Seven words that are exact on the lens lower the off-lattice count.
- **What the commutator does, piece by piece** (`comm_anatomy.py`, exact; `results/comm-anatomy-I_a-40-stop.txt`). Both twists of the word above are jumble twists. A piece caught by a twist and by its inverse, and by no other twist of the word, comes back unchanged: 2,401 pieces (caught by the twists of 30 only) and 2,702 (of 145 only). The fragment of 164 pieces is caught by the first, third and fourth twists, so its net change is the last twist alone, (145, 33), which puts it on the lattice. 93 lattice pieces are caught by the first three, net change a⁻¹qa, and leave the lattice. No piece is caught by all four twists of this word. A piece caught by all four gets the group commutator q⁻¹a⁻¹qa, which is not the identity in general (I_a from the solved state, word (0, 1)(1, 3)(0, 1)(1, 9): 954 pieces are caught by all four twists and all 954 move). So a commutator changes the pieces whose membership in R_c or R_d changes between a twist and its inverse, and the pieces caught by all four twists; it is clean when every such piece belongs to a fragment that the net change returns. Here the net is 164 − 93 = 71 pieces returned.
- **E2: no history** (`e2_frag.py`). A solver given only the stop configuration, with no record of its own, the potential P = (off-lattice count, N) in lexicographic order and the lens commutator as an extra rule; every accepted step must lower P, so the run cannot cycle. Result: stuck at P = (690, 1,080) after 7 accepted steps and 4.7 hours (3 commutators, which lower the off-lattice count by 71, 94 and 5, and 4 conjugate repairs, which lower N). Every step was found without any record. At the end 690 pieces are off the lattice in six fragments (424, 93, 63, 50, 48 and 12 pieces), and no rule lowers P: in particular none of the 22,656 lens commutators the rule enumerates there. In the run the off-lattice count fell only through commutators. Step log: `results/joint-attack-I_a-40.json`.

Part B of the solver's proposal (reopen the solver's own repair words until a carrier exists, then return it) was not adopted as the method. It depends on the solver's history, so it cannot restore a configuration handed over without one. Its stopping rule can return to an earlier state and loop. At this stop it stops after 13 reversals, not 27.

Owner decision (10 October 2026, [record](../../../docs/wiki/decisions/owner-decisions-2026-10-10-jumbling-complete.md)): a guarantee limited to shallow scrambles (for example 20 twists) is not acceptable; R1 closes only with a method that restores every reachable configuration.

Results of 11 October 2026 (`deep_scramble.py`, `results/deep-scramble-I_a.json`):
- **Lattice-prefix rule** (proved; checked exactly). Let G be a lattice configuration and L a twist sequence admissible from the solved state, reaching W. Then L is admissible from G, and every piece p ends in the pose W[s(p)] · G[p], where s(p) is the piece whose home place p occupies in G. Proof: K+ maps retained chambers onto retained chambers, so in G every posed piece region is the home region of a piece of the same orbit and G has the posed regions of the solved state; whether a twist is admissible, and which pieces it moves, depends only on the posed regions; induction along L. Check: 60 random retained twists, then 60 random admissible twists (50 jumble and 10 retained), 0 exact mismatches over all pieces. Consequence: a retained scramble placed before a jumble scramble changes only which piece sits where. The off-lattice count, the domains and N are those of the jumble scramble alone.
- **Inscribed 600-cells.** The 600 grip poles split into five inscribed 600-cells of 120 poles each (neighbours 36 degrees apart). The caps of one of them hold 97.02% of the pieces (171,840 of 177,120). From the solved state, no twist of a pole blocks any of its 12 neighbours in the same set (I_a: every pole of one set, every non-identity element). Rounds of one twist per pole of a set, each pole taking the non-identity menu element, retained or jumble, that leaves the most pieces off the lattice, give 90.8% of the pieces off the lattice after one round (120 twists, all jumble) and 93.5% after three (360 twists: 145 jumble and 215 retained; rounds 2 and 3 take 22 and 3 jumble twists), with no twist blocked. Random admissible scrambles stay at about two thirds (I_a, 63 to 69% from 700 to 1,400 twists), and a greedy choice over random grips reached 76% after 300 twists (scratch runs, not kept). From the solved state each jumble twist blocks 25 grips, all 16 nearest among them (retained twists block none), so random twists leave lattice islands that no free grip reaches.
- Use: the 93.5% sample is the deep test input for the observation views ([jumbling-observation](../../../docs/progress/1.0/jumbling-observation.md), section 9) and a restoration input far deeper than the W-J fixtures. - **Five ordinary puzzles inside I_a** (`classes.py`, exact; `results/classes-I_a.json`; full proofs requested in [card 3](cards/card-3-hidden-puzzles.md)). Computed: write points of R⁴ as quaternions, so that K⁺ = {x ↦ u x w⁻¹ : u, w ∈ 2I}. The five inscribed 600-cells above are the classes n_c·2I (n_c the pole of grip c), and K⁺ permutes them as A5. At every grip the I_a menu is {x ↦ (n̂_c u n̂_c⁻¹) x u⁻¹ : u ∈ 2I}, the stabiliser of n̂_c in Sym⁺(P), the rotation group of the 600-cell P of its own class; some menu element maps a pole of any other class off the poles. Sym⁺(P) meets exactly five domains: the lattice and four others, the frames of P, each at left tree distance 2. The 20 frames of the five classes are distinct, and the cuts of one class divide the puzzle into 1,441 cells. Proof sketch (integrator, not reviewed as mathematics): a piece in a frame of P has pose h·k with h ∈ Sym⁺(P) and k ∈ K⁺, and its region straddles the cut of a grip d of P only if the chamber k(Ā₀) straddles the cut of the grip whose pole is h⁻¹n_d, again a pole of P; no chamber straddles a cut. So pieces on the lattice or in frames of P never block a grip of P, and twists of P alone act on the cells of P's cuts as an ordinary vertex-turning puzzle on that 600-cell. Its outline P̃ is not Sym⁺(P)-invariant, so that puzzle changes shape (a shape mod). And since frames of different classes are distinct, a single twist puts pieces of a frame of P back on the lattice only if its grip belongs to P. Observed (every off-lattice piece lies in a frame): at the I_a@40 stop, fragments of 424, 164 and 12 pieces (class 0), 197 (class 1) and 63 (class 2); at the E2 end, 424 and 12 (class 0), 50 and 48 (class 1), 63 (class 2) and 93 (class 4). At the deep scramble the four off-lattice domains are the four frames of class 0, and the 480 blocked grips are exactly those of classes 1 to 4. No piece of a frame straddles a cut of its own class at any of the three. Consequence for R1 (lead): no word of twists of its own class returns a fragment that shares a cell with lattice pieces while keeping those lattice pieces on the lattice (card 3, D1), and no single twist of another class puts any of its pieces on the lattice, so a clean return needs words that mix classes (card 3, E).
