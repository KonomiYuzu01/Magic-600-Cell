## A finite solver with a complete proof inside the domain

Given a fixed nonnegative integer k, enumerate all legal words of length at most k from the current state, preserving complete labelled endpoints. For each reached endpoint Y, test the three entrance predicates. On the first success, construct that entrance's bounded restoration, prepend the enumerated word, and verify the complete literal certificate. If all finite candidates fail, return OUTSIDE_CERTIFIED_DOMAIN(k), not unreachable and not unsolvable.

The solving domain D[k] consists exactly of reachable inputs within k legal letters of the union of the three geometric entrances. The finite admission search terminates on every input in the generated universe. Under the reachability promise, the complete construction terminates, accepts exactly D[k], and emits a legal sticker-solving word on acceptance. Without that promise, a geometric entrance match need not be reachable, and neither successful sticker synthesis nor termination of the promised-domain lattice constructor is asserted. Reversibility is not used to guess an unknown input history; the prefix is found in an explicitly bounded search and all entrance recognition is finite.

Let h be the maximum initial piece height. A literal k-prefix has every height at most h+2k. If it enters Gamma(A), the reference target height H at that endpoint is at most h+2k. Hence a valid uniform length bound on D[k] is

    U(h,k) = max( k+C, k+T, h/2+2k+20892764 ).

Every constructed path has peak height at most

    E(h,k) = max( h+2k, 4024 ).

These estimates include the prefix, all preparations, all temporary collateral and the actual lattice sticker phase. They do not assert that U is sharp or that the finite searches are efficient.

## What would make this universal

A proved computable k(h,...) that places every reachable state inside D[k(h,...)] would convert the explicit U/E formulas into a global quantitative theorem. No such bound or admission theorem is currently known. Taking an unbounded union over k covers the reachable component because it contains solved, but unboundedly increasing k only recovers a terminating promised-domain search, not a new computable uniform upper bound. This is the same distinction as the existing M* and source computability equivalence.
