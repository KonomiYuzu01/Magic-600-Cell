## Why the second half is legal

This argument does not rest on "Q2 does not move the old off pieces", on the literal mirror order of P2^-1, or on the
K-membership of Q2's letters. Q2 contains X, which is outside K+.

1. **The restored 17-block is ordinary lattice content.** At E1 the 17 pieces restored in segment 1 have K poses
   (`end-table.json` of segment 1). Each sits in a chamber like any other lattice piece. Neither the menu nor the ghost
   treats them differently.
2. **Bad(W2) covers every lattice piece, and V2 empties it.** Bad(W2) is computed from W2 alone (Proposition S) by
   the chamber ghost over every chamber of W2's caps. A chamber lies outside Bad(W2) exactly when a lattice piece in
   it never straddles a cut along W2 and ends on K. V2 is SAFE-ALL at E1, so it moves no off piece, and it leaves
   Bad(W2) vacant; this was checked on the state after V2, before W2. So at the start of W2 every lattice piece, the
   17 included, is in Good(W2).
3. **The off pieces are followed exactly.** The frame ghost follows each off piece as its own content, and a content
   dies at the first twist whose cut it straddles. Before construction:
   - T is alive at the end of W2 and on K (87 of 87);
   - B' is alive at the end at its exact contents (`pre.json`: `T_on_lattice_at_end`, `protected_exact_at_end`).

   So no off piece straddles any cut at any twist of W2. The replay adds that B' is never selected
   (`window-E1.json`).
4. **Every twist is admissible.** A twist is admissible exactly when no piece straddles its cut. By 2 and 3, no piece
   straddles at any twist of W2. So every twist is admissible and the end of W2 is the ghost's prediction.
   - In particular, P2^-1 is admissible after Q2, and its real selections are the predicted ones. Pieces outside the
     net-moved set M follow the mirror of P2 (F2). M = T follows its own ghost from its landing, which lies in
     Good(P2^-1) (F3).
   - Q2 does select parked pieces: 197 of the 592, at 4 of its letters. It returns every one exactly, so its
     net-moved set is T alone. The inverse part is therefore applied to a state that differs from the state after P2
     only on T.
5. **V2^-1 is admissible after W2.** A letter's SAFE-ALL status depends only on the off pieces. After W2 the off set
   is B' alone, with unchanged exact poses, a subset of the off set during V2. So each letter of V2^-1 is admissible.
   Its K+ elements move T's pieces as lattice pieces, which stay on K.

The replay confirms every point (`window-E1.json`): F1 holds for 177,033 pieces, and F2 and F3 hold. P2^-1 selects T
at 6 letters, where the mirror letters of P2 selected none of T.

**Lemma H' (lower bound).** The model, as the simulator implements it:
- (M1) A piece's region is its exact pose applied to its home chamber. The piece is on K exactly when its pose is in
  K+, and then its region is a chamber.
- (M2) Chambers are the cells of the 600 cuts, so no chamber straddles a cut.
- (M3) Every menu element of grip g fixes the pole n_g (36,000 of 36,000, `holes/polefix.json`). So a twist maps the
  closed half-space of its cap onto itself and fixes the other closed half-space.
- (M4) A twist is admissible when no piece straddles its cut. It moves exactly the pieces inside the cap.

From (M3), an admissible twist keeps any two non-straddling regions with disjoint interiors disjoint: two regions on
the same side get the same isometry or none, and two regions on opposite sides stay in opposite closed half-spaces.
The solved state's regions are the chambers, so piece regions have disjoint interiors at every reachable state. They
need not cover the body: rotating a cap by an element outside K+ does not map the polytope onto itself.

Statement. Let U be admissible from a state Y, and let S be a set of pieces that are off at Y and on K after U. Then
|Good(U)| + |S| <= 177,120, that is, |Bad(U)| >= |S|.

Proof.
1. Let F be the regions of the chambers in Good(U), as lattice contents, together with the regions of the pieces
   of S at Y.
2. The members of F have disjoint interiors at Y. Two chambers do, and two pieces do. Suppose the content of a good
   chamber x and a piece p of S overlap.
   - Neither ever straddles along U: x is good, and p is a real piece under an admissible word.
   - Two overlapping regions that do not straddle a cut lie on the same side of it. So at every twist both get the
     same isometry or none, they still overlap afterwards, and after U both have been multiplied by the same product h.
   - x is good, so its content ends on K: h k is in K+ for its K+ pose k, so h is in K+.
   - p's pose is m_p, not in K+, so h m_p is not in K+ and p ends off K. That contradicts p in S.
3. No member of F ever straddles along U, so by (M3) the members keep disjoint interiors to the end.
4. At the end every member is on K, so its region is a chamber. Members with disjoint interiors are distinct
   chambers. So F maps injectively into the 177,120 chambers. ∎

  the holes; the holes number the off pieces; and the lemma with S = all off pieces gives the reverse count.

it is false, and the owner's own record shows it:
- The owner's word restores the 87-block first: window 36568 takes 36563 (107 off) to 36593, with 20 off pieces, the
  17-block and B'.
- From 36593 the owner's word restores the 17-block cleanly: the 1,957 twists to 38550 put the 17 pieces on K, every
  lattice piece of 36593 is on K at 38550, and the off set at 38550 is B'. |Bad(U)| = |Bad(U^-1)| = 17
  (`audit/math/owner36593.json`, recomputed from the owner's word).
- 36593 is reachable from X: X is 36563 followed by 302 K+ letters u (C-X.md), so u^-1 and then the 30-twist window
  reach 36593 (`audit/skeptic-MATH01/chain.json`). The K+ letters keep every piece's lattice membership at every
  prefix. The window does not: its first twist (491,56) takes 3,009 lattice pieces of 36563 off K, and up to 5,907 are
  off inside it. At 36593 every lattice piece of 36563 is on K again (`<theory>/results/outside/unlock-e2-to-lattice.json`,
  window 36568; `audit/skeptic-MATH01/transient.json`). Lattice preservation holds at the end of the window, not at
  every prefix; growth of the off set in the middle is allowed.
- A successor of X in the same strict sense as E1 also has such a word. Y* = u(36593) (digest `c35d699a…`) differs
  from X only in the 87-block, which is on K; the 17-block and B' keep their exact X poses; it has 20 off pieces and
  20 holes. At Y* the word u^-1 U (2,259 twists) is admissible, restores the 17-block and leaves no lattice piece of Y*
  off at the end. It is not pose-preserving: B' ends at a different exact pose, and 91,192 lattice pieces of 36593
  change exact pose while staying on K (`audit/skeptic-MATH01/chain.json`).

What holds is an ordering and resource limit of the route used here. For W = P Q P^-1 of the found shape, vacated by a
SAFE-ALL word, the 17-block words found at X have |Bad(W)| >= 87 (exactly 87 for the 23 parked words carried
furthest). Restoring the 87-block first leaves 20 holes, fewer than 87. So this route, with these words, cannot then
restore the 17-block.

The necessary conditions, by (U1) and (U2) above: let Y be a successor with 20 off pieces, the 17-block and B',
with 20 holes.
- A clean word U at Y needs Bad(U) inside Y's 20 holes.
- A SAFE-ALL conjugate V W V^-1 has |Bad| = |Bad(W)|.
- So the 17-block words found at X are excluded at Y, with every SAFE-ALL conjugate, since |Bad| >= 87 > 20.
- By Lemma H' with S = the 17-block, any clean U at Y that restores the 17-block has 17 <= |Bad(U)| <= 20. If it
  restores B' as well, Bad(U) is exactly Y's 20 holes.

Words with 17 <= |Bad| <= 20 are not excluded, and neither are other preparations or families. At 36593 one exists
(the owner's, with |Bad| = 17), and at Y* one exists (u^-1 U). Not searched: a clean word at such a successor that
also keeps B''s exact pose (the owner's U moves B'), and any word at the 20-off successor that this report's route
would reach by restoring the 87-block first from X.

N1 and N2 are necessary conditions only. The finite search results and the G3 observations are instance data, not
general sufficiency.
